\noindent This section justifies the choice of association coefficient used throughout
Q~D. The counter-example of Section~\ref{sec:contre} is produced by
\texttt{scripts/contre\_exemple\_kendall.py}, which computes both coefficients twice ---
once from their definition, once through \texttt{scipy} --- so that each controls the
other. All census figures come from the campaign of 2\,000 runs.

\section{Three Features of the Data, and What They Rule Out}

The quantities to be correlated are durations measured on a finite horizon
$H = 2\,000$. Three features of the data bear on the choice of coefficient.

\paragraph{Non-linearity, and worse.} The median $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $118$ steps at
$\Delta e = 0.028$, rises to $346$ at $\Delta e = 0.085$, then falls steadily to $28$ at
the top of the grid: a range of $28$ to $346$, a factor of $12.4$, and \textbf{not
monotone in $\Delta e$}. The relationship is therefore not merely curved, which would
already understate Pearson's $r$; it is non-monotone, so no single linear coefficient
summarises it and its sign depends on which part of the grid is included. This is
consistent with what is established elsewhere in this work about the power-law fit of the
article, whose measured-to-predicted ratio is $0.19$ at the lowest amplitude and $2.08$ at
$\Delta e = 0.141$: the fit holds only in the middle of the grid.

\paragraph{Extreme values.} At low amplitude the durations are both large and highly
dispersed. Pearson's coefficient is a ratio of moments and is not robust: a handful of
runs in the tail move it substantially, whereas both rank coefficients are unaffected.

\paragraph{Administrative censoring at $H$.} A duration that is never reached within the
horizon has no numerical value. $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ is censored in $9.10\%$ of
runs overall and in up to $78\%$ at a single amplitude; $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ at
$\lambda = 50$ is censored in $99.95\%$ of runs.

\paragraph{Which of the three survives stratification.} The subject invites the reader to
check what remains once $\Delta e$ is held constant within a stratum. \emph{Non-linearity
does not survive}: the curvature under discussion is curvature \emph{in $\Delta e$}, and
within a stratum $\Delta e$ is fixed by construction. The other two do survive, and they
are not objections of the same kind. Extreme values make Pearson's coefficient
\textbf{inefficient} --- it can still be computed, it is simply unstable and moves with a
few runs. Censoring makes it \textbf{undefined} --- a censored run has no numerical value
to enter the sum of products, so the coefficient cannot literally be formed without first
inventing one. The second objection is the one that carries the argument: the first is a
matter of degree, the second is a matter of definition. It is also the objection that
disqualifies Pearson on our data specifically, since $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ is
censored in nearly four runs out of five at $\Delta e = 0.085$.

Both rank coefficients handle censoring the same way, and for the same reason. The horizon
$H$ is \emph{identical} for every run, so a censored duration genuinely exceeds every
observed one. Censored runs may therefore be placed at the last rank, tied among
themselves. This argument would collapse if the horizon varied from run to run, in which
case a censored value would only be known to exceed \emph{its own} horizon and no ranking
would follow. That is the point at which the choice between Spearman and Kendall becomes
decisive.

\section{Definition of $\tau_b$}

For $n$ pairs, let $C$ be the number of concordant pairs, $D$ the number of discordant
pairs, and $n_0 = n(n-1)/2$. Let $t_x$ be the number of pairs tied on $x$ only, $t_y$ the
number tied on $y$ only, and pairs tied on both sides counted in each. Then
\begin{equation}
  \tau_b = \frac{C - D}{\sqrt{(n_0 - t_x)(n_0 - t_y)}}.
  \label{eq:taub}
\end{equation}
The denominator is what distinguishes $\tau_b$ from the ordinary $\tau_a$: each tie block
is removed from the count on the side where it occurs, so the coefficient is normalised by
the number of pairs that could in principle have been ordered on both sides. Spearman's
$\rho$ has no such correction. It is Pearson's coefficient computed on mid-ranks, and a
block of ties simply receives a common mid-rank which then enters a sum of squares as
though it were an ordinary value.

\section{The Two Tie Structures, Treated Separately}

The comparators of Q~D do not all present the same tie structure, and the consequence is
not the same in the two cases.

\paragraph{Ties on both sides: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$.}
Both are durations and both can be censored at $H$. Censored runs form a tie block on the
$y$ side; $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself is never censored ($0.0000$), but it is discrete
and takes only $321$ distinct values over $2\,000$ runs, so it carries tie blocks of its
own. Here \eqref{eq:taub} corrects on both sides and the compensation is \emph{complete}:
the coefficient is normalised by exactly the set of pairs that were orderable.

\paragraph{Ties on one side: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$.}
These two quantities are defined on every run and are \emph{never} censored: $2\,000$
values out of $2\,000$, verified, with $1\,389$ and $1\,544$ distinct values respectively.
There is no tie block on their side at all. The correction in \eqref{eq:taub} then applies
to the $x$ side only. The compensation is still exact in the sense that the denominator
counts the orderable pairs, but the two sides are no longer symmetric: ties in
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ remove pairs from the numerator without any counterpart, so the
coefficient is displaced \emph{towards zero} relative to what a tie-free measurement of
the same association would give. The direction matters for reading Q~D.3: the coefficients
reported there for $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$ are conservative, and a value indistinguishable
from zero cannot be an artefact of ties inflating it.

\section{The Counter-Example, and an Error in the Prescribed Exercise}
\label{sec:contre}

Ten fictitious runs on a common horizon $H = 2\,000$: four observed, six censored. The
exercise as prescribed asks for the four observed values to be \emph{perfectly ordered}.
Carried out literally --- with the censored runs tied on both sides --- it yields
$\rho = \tau_b = 1.000000$ exactly. The gap is zero and the counter-example demonstrates
nothing.

The reason is worth stating precisely, because it is sharper than ``the example is badly
chosen''. Ties on both sides require $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself to be censored. On this
campaign it never is: $\texttt{censored\_arf} = 0.0000$ on all $2\,000$ runs. The
configuration under which the prescribed exercise produces no gap is one that cannot occur
in the data it is meant to illustrate.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    Configuration & $\rho$ (Spearman) & $\tau_b$ (Kendall) & gap $\rho - \tau_b$ \\
    \midrule
    Prescribed, read literally (ties both sides) & $1.000000$ & $1.000000$ & $0.000000$ \\
    Observed values ordered, $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ uncensored & $0.887625$ & $0.816497$ & $0.071129$ \\
    Observed values anti-ordered & $0.751068$ & $0.489898$ & $0.261170$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Ten runs, four observed and six censored at $H$. Each coefficient is computed
  from its definition and by \texttt{scipy}; the two agree to $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ or
  better in every cell, which is the control on the calculation.}
  \label{tab:contre}
\end{table}

Two things separate the three rows of Table~\ref{tab:contre}, and they should not be
conflated. What removes the gap entirely is the \emph{tie structure}: as soon as
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is left uncensored --- the situation that actually holds --- the two
coefficients part company even with the observed runs perfectly ordered, by $0.071$. What
then \emph{amplifies} the gap is the ordering of the observed runs: with four anti-ordered
observations, Spearman reports $0.751$ where $\tau_b$ reports $0.490$, a gap of $0.261$,
nearly four times the ordered case.

The mechanism is the same in both rows. Spearman overstates the association because the
six censored runs share a mid-rank which then enters its sums of squares as an ordinary
value, manufacturing agreement out of a tie block; $\tau_b$ removes those pairs from both
counts instead. The prescribed exercise hides this because it silently assumes ties on
both sides, which cancels the effect exactly.

This is reported as a correction to the prescribed exercise, not as a private difficulty:
the exercise as written cannot exhibit the effect it is designed to exhibit, and the
condition under which it fails is precisely the one that does not hold in the campaign.

\section{Conclusion}

Pearson is excluded by definition, not by degree: censored runs carry no numerical value.
Spearman is computable but overstates the association in the presence of tie blocks, and
the tie blocks here are created by the censoring itself. Kendall's $\tau_b$ is used
throughout Q~D, with censored runs imputed to the last rank --- legitimate because the
horizon is common to all runs, and only because of that.
