\noindent This section fixes the rule by which a coefficient of Q~D is called discernible,
reports censoring by amplitude rather than in aggregate, and settles whether censoring
drives any of the results. All figures come from the campaign of 2\,000 runs and are
reproduced by \texttt{scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} and \texttt{scripts/derives\_QD.py}.

\section{A Reservation on the Status of the Criterion}

The protocol asks for a decision criterion written down before any correlation is
inspected. That claim cannot honestly be made here, and the reservation is stated rather
than glossed.

The pre-registered document of this work fixes the criteria of a \emph{benchmark} --- the
tests A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, D1--D3 that qualify candidate replacements for
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against an analytic reference. It contains neither the indicator
evaluated here, nor its comparators, nor the decision domain $\Delta e \ge 0.10$, nor the
threshold on $\tau_b$, nor the rule on confidence-interval bounds. The columns
\texttt{dans\_domaine\_decision}, \texttt{seuil\_detectabilite} and \texttt{discernable}
are produced by the \emph{same} analysis pass as the coefficients they qualify. Anteriority
is therefore \textbf{not} claimed for the criterion of Q~D. It is fixed here, applied
uniformly, and no cell is reclassified afterwards.

\section{Two Incompatible Rules, and the One That Governs}

Two rules for calling an association discernible coexist in this work.

\begin{description}
  \item[Threshold rule.] $|\tau_b| > 0.13295637$, the value in the
    \texttt{seuil\_detectabilite} column, constant across the table.
  \item[Interval rule.] The bootstrap confidence interval excludes zero. A $\tau_b$ whose
    interval contains zero does not count.
\end{description}

They disagree on exactly three cells out of the $108$ in the decision domain:

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
    \toprule
    $\Delta e$ & comparator & $\tau_b$ & CI & threshold rule & interval rule \\
    \midrule
    $0.287$ & $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ & $+0.1363$ & $[-0.0068 ; +0.2704]$ & discernible & not \\
    $0.287$ & $S_{\max}(H)$                & $+0.1308$ & $[+0.0027 ; +0.2519]$ & not & discernible \\
    $0.391$ & $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ & $+0.1240$ & $[+0.0010 ; +0.2505]$ & not & discernible \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{The three cells on which the two rules disagree.}
  \label{tab:regles}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The interval rule governs.} Three reasons, and they are not of equal weight.
First, a fixed threshold on $|\tau_b|$ ignores the precision of the estimate: it treats a
coefficient of $0.13$ measured on $100$ seeds with a wide interval exactly like one
measured tightly, whereas the interval rule uses the information the bootstrap actually
provides. Second, the threshold is a function of $n$ alone, so it says nothing about the
tie structure of the particular pair being tested --- and Q~D.2 established that the tie
structure differs systematically between the $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$ comparators and the
never-censored $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$. Third, the pre-registered benchmark of this work
already uses the interval rule for test B1, so adopting it here keeps one rule across the
whole project instead of two.

This rule is applied throughout Q~D, and the counts of Q~D.3 are those it gives:
$A(H)$ discernible at $0$ of $18$ amplitudes --- unchanged, since the two rules agree on
every $A(H)$ cell --- and $S_{\max}(H)$ at $\mathbf{15}$ of $18$. The
\texttt{discernable} column of the table implements the threshold rule and would give
$14$: the difference is the single cell $\Delta e = 0.287$, whose interval
$[+0.0027 ; +0.2519]$ excludes zero while its $\tau_b$ of $0.1308$ falls just under the
threshold. The count reported throughout Q~D is $15$, and the column is not used as the
criterion. None of the three cells of Table~\ref{tab:regles} is quoted individually as a
result.

\section{Why Censored Runs May Be Ranked Last}

The horizon $H = 2\,000$ is identical for every run. A duration not reached within it
therefore genuinely exceeds every observed duration, and censored runs may be placed at
the last rank, tied among themselves. Kendall's $\tau_b$ then removes those pairs from the
count on the side where the tie occurs, as Q~D.2 sets out.

The argument depends entirely on the horizon being common. If $H$ varied from run to run,
a censored value would only be known to exceed \emph{its own} horizon: a run censored at
$H = 500$ and an observed run of $1\,200$ steps could not be ordered at all, and no
imputation to a shared last rank would be licit. Under variable horizons the correct tool
is a censoring-aware estimator such as Harrell's $C$ index, not an imputation. The
convention used here is legitimate because of a property of this experimental design, and
it does not transfer.

Aggregate censoring rates conceal what matters.

  \caption{Censoring overall, at its worst amplitude on the full grid, and at its worst
  amplitude inside the decision domain. The two columns differ because the two amplitudes
  below $\Delta e = 0.10$ carry most of the censoring, and they are outside the domain on
  which the criterion is declared. Baseline estimated over $3\,000$ pre-drift steps.}
  \label{tab:censure}

\paragraph{$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ fails the group's own criterion.} The criterion
adopted for the benchmark of this work requires censoring at most $5\%$ \emph{over the
declared domain}. Inside the decision domain, $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ reaches $54\%$
at $\Delta e = 0.141$, exceeding the criterion by a factor of $10.8$. Its worst value on
the full grid is $78\%$, at $\Delta e = 0.085$ --- but that amplitude lies \emph{outside}
the decision domain, and it is quoted here as a grid figure, not as a breach of an
in-domain criterion. Its coefficients are published in Q~D.3 because suppressing them
would hide the comparator that most stresses the method, but they are published \emph{with
this failure stated}: at $\Delta e = 0.141$, the lowest amplitude of the decision domain,
the $\tau_b$ of $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ rests on a minority of observed runs and is
not quoted as evidence.

\paragraph{No median delay beyond $50\%$ censoring.} At $\lambda = 25$ and $\lambda = 50$
no median detection delay is reported anywhere in Q~D, and at $\lambda = 8$ none is
reported at $\Delta e = 0.028$ where censoring reaches $90\%$. A median computed on the
surviving runs excludes the slowest ones by construction and is not a median of the
quantity of interest.

\section{Does Censoring Drive the Results?}

Each coefficient was recomputed twice: once with censored runs imputed to the last rank,
once on complete cases only. The two are compared cell by cell.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lc}
    \toprule
    Comparator & max $|$imputed $-$ complete cases$|$ \\
    \midrule
    $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(10\%)$, $A(H)$, $S_{\max}(H)$ & $0.000000$ \\
    $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(25\%)$ & $0.000228$ \\
    $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(50\%)$ & $0.030591$ \\
    $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ & $0.040259$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Sensitivity of $\tau_b$ to the treatment of censored runs.}
  \label{tab:cascomplets}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Answer: no.} For the three never-censored comparators the two computations agree
exactly, to $0.000000$, which is what they must do and serves as a control on the
implementation. For the two comparators that are materially censored the largest
disagreement anywhere in the table is $0.0403$, on $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ --- an
order of magnitude below the coefficients that carry the conclusions of Q~D.3, and well
inside their confidence intervals. \textbf{Censoring does not drive any result of
question~D.} The imputation convention could be replaced by complete-case analysis without
changing a single verdict.

One correction to an earlier internal note: the claim that imputed and complete-case
values ``agree to three decimal places'' is too strong. It holds for
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(25\%)$ and for the never-censored comparators, not for
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(50\%)$ nor $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$, where the agreement is to
two decimal places.

\section{The Blind Spot, Quantified}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    $\lambda$ & runs that alarm & silent & $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ \\
    \midrule
    $8$  & $1\,906 / 2\,000$ & $4.70\%$  & $95.44\%$ \\
    $25$ & $778 / 2\,000$    & $61.10\%$ & $6.56\%$ \\
    $50$ & $1 / 2\,000$      & $99.95\%$ & $0.00\%$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Alarm behaviour by threshold. The last column is computed on uncensored runs
  only, and is the race of question~A read from this campaign.}
  \label{tab:aveugle}
\end{table}

At $\lambda = 50$ a single run out of $2\,000$ ever alarms. The methodological consequence
is immediate and is the reason the campaign fixes no $\lambda$: with $99.95\%$ censoring,
\emph{no} correlation involving $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is computable at that threshold. Had
a threshold been frozen inside the simulation loop, as an earlier prototype did, the
resulting table would have carried a $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ column that is empty in all but
one row.

The last column sharpens the picture. The blind spot is not a fixed property of the
apparatus: at $\lambda = 8$ the detector wins the race in $95.44\%$ of runs, and at
$\lambda = 25$ in $6.56\%$. The transition between ``the detector is never late'' and
``the detector never fires'' happens entirely between $\lambda = 8$ and $\lambda = 50$,
which is a narrow band of the tuning parameter.

\section{Effect of the Baseline Window}

Since $\hat p_0$ enters $S_t$ directly, every censoring rate above depends on the window
used to estimate it. Both windows required by the protocol are reported in Q~D.1: mean
censoring of $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ moves from $0.0405$ to $0.0470$ at $\lambda = 8$, from
$0.6045$ to $0.6110$ at $\lambda = 25$, and from $0.9990$ to $0.9995$ at $\lambda = 50$
when the window goes from $1\,000$ to $3\,000$ steps. The shifts are below one point and
change no verdict of this section, but they are reported because the protocol requires
both windows and because the direction is systematic: a longer window gives a slightly
higher baseline, hence slightly less accumulated evidence and slightly fewer alarms.

\section{Not Addressed}

No result of Q~D is established as a function of $M$: the instrumented campaign exists at
$M = 10$ only. No result involves $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ at $\lambda = 25$ or $\lambda = 50$
as a correlated variable, for the censoring reason given above. Harrell's $C$ index was
not used, because the common-horizon argument makes rank imputation licit here and the
complete-case comparison shows the choice is immaterial; under a variable horizon that
reasoning would not hold and the $C$ index would be required.
