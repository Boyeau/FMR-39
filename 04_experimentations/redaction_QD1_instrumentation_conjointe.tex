\noindent All measurements come from the instrumented campaign of 2\,000 runs
(20 amplitudes $\times$ 100 seeds, $H=2\,000$, $M=10$ trees) in
\texttt{04\_experimentations/resultats\_R2/}. Every figure quoted is reproduced by
\texttt{scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} and \texttt{scripts/derives\_QD.py}. The hierarchy
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$ of Q~C.1 is assumed established.

\section{What Is Recorded and What Is Recomputed}

The simulation writes raw material only: the error trajectory $e_t \in \{0,1\}$, the tree
replacement events, and the $3\,000$ pre-drift steps. No threshold and no $\lambda$ appears
anywhere in the simulation loop.

That is deliberate. The external detector reads the error trajectory and never acts on the
forest, so $\lambda$ has no influence on the dynamics. Once the \texttt{break} is removed,
the three scenarios of R2 produce runs identical to the bit. One campaign replaces three,
and $S_t$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$ are swept offline for every
threshold and any $\delta_P$. Freezing a threshold inside the loop would have cost one
campaign per threshold, and at $\lambda = 50$ it would have produced a table whose
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ column is censored in $99.95\%$ of runs.

\section{The Three Series on a Common Time Axis}

  \caption{Fraction of replaced trees $N_t/M$ (inter-seed median and interquartile range),
  global error $\bar e_t$, and internal detector state $S_t$ on a common time axis, at
  three amplitudes spanning the grid, with the four temporal markers. The time axis is
  logarithmic: the four instants span two orders of magnitude across the grid, and on a
  linear axis the markers of the high-amplitude column collapse into the first few pixels.
  The $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ marker is dashed where the test lacks the power to resolve it.}
  \label{fig:joint}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{No temporal smoothing.} $e_t$ is binary. The only averaging applied is the
inter-seed mean, taken step by step, with no sliding filter anywhere. A moving average of
length $L$ shifts the curve by half its support and destroys the synchronisation with
$\tau^*$, which is what this figure exists to display. At the top of the grid the median
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $28$ steps, so a $50$-step filter would displace the curve by
nearly the whole quantity being measured.

\paragraph{The four markers.} $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is plotted at $\lambda = 8$, the only
threshold whose censoring stays in the minority ($4.70\%$, against $61.10\%$ at
$\lambda = 25$ and $99.95\%$ at $\lambda = 50$). At the other two thresholds a median
delay would rest on a minority of runs, so none is published. $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is the
return of the mean error below $\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$ with $\rho = 0.25$, held for
$20$ consecutive steps; Q~C.1 writes it $\tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$, and the two names
denote the same object throughout. The persistence requirement does real work: without it,
two amplitudes cross at the very first post-drift step, which is a fluctuation rather than
a recovery, and two more cross at step $101$, well before the median
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, without the crossing being sustained.

\paragraph{Replacement dynamics.} At $t = 100$ the median $N_t/M$ ranges from $0.00$ to
$0.70$ across amplitudes, with an interquartile range of $0.00$ to $0.30$; at $t = 400$,
from $0.10$ to $0.90$. By the end of the horizon the median runs from $0.60$ to $1.00$,
and the proportion of runs whose forest has been entirely renewed goes from $2\%$ to
$94\%$ depending on amplitude.

\section{The Baseline on Two Windows}

The baseline $\hat p_0$ governs $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$, hence
every censoring rate reported in Q~D.4. It is therefore reported on both windows the
protocol requires: $1\,000$ pre-drift steps, reproducing R2 as published, and $3\,000$
steps for the analysis. Since $3\,000$ pre-drift steps are recorded per run, this is a
purely offline recomputation. As a control, the baseline recomputed at $3\,000$ steps
reproduces the campaign value with a maximum deviation of exactly $0$.

The window shifts the censoring rates by less than one point and changes no verdict. The
direction of the shift is systematic: a baseline estimated over $3\,000$ steps is slightly
higher, so the detector accumulates slightly less evidence and alarms slightly less often.

\paragraph{Against the theoretical baseline.} The pre-drift target
$y = \mathbf{1}\{x_0 + x_1 > 0\}$ is a deterministic function of the inputs. There is no
label noise and the Bayes error is exactly $0$, so the measured baseline of $0.023$ is
entirely approximation error, produced by axis-parallel splits approximating an oblique
boundary. The detector monitors departures from a reference level that belongs to the
classifier rather than to the problem.

\section{What Is Acquired at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$}

Two quantities are measured at the instant $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$: the fraction of
adaptation already acquired, $R(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$, and the fraction of evidence
already accumulated by the detector, $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$. $G$ is computed run by run,
$R$ per amplitude on the mean curve, and $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is the inter-seed median.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{resultats_R2/resultats/figures/Fig_QD_R_et_G_full.png}
  \caption{$R(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$ and $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$ against $\Delta e$.
  Neither is monotone, and $R$ is negative at five amplitudes.}
  \label{fig:rg}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Answer.} $G$ ranges from $0.4889$ to $0.7594$ over the 18 amplitudes of the
decision domain: between half and three quarters of the evidence is already available to
the detector when the first tree is replaced. On the full grid it falls to $0.1023$, and
that restriction is published with the figure.

$R$ supports no such single sentence. It runs from $-0.1882$ to $+0.8853$ on the grid,
from $-0.1882$ to $+0.5883$ on the decision domain, and it is not monotone in $\Delta e$.
At $\Delta e = 0.085$ it reaches $0.885$: nearly all of the adaptation is acquired when the
first tree is replaced, the opposite of what one would expect. At $\Delta e = 0.141$ it is
$0.588$. The reading ``early in the adaptation, late in the race'' is not supported by the
table and is not used here. What the measurement supports is narrower: at
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, between $49\%$ and $76\%$ of the evidence is accumulated, while the
fraction of adaptation acquired varies from negative to $89\%$ with no monotone dependence
on amplitude.

\paragraph{A negative fraction of adaptation.} $R$ is negative at five amplitudes
($\Delta e = 0.243$, $0.436$, $0.494$, $0.496$, $0.498$), reaching $-0.1882$. The window
used to estimate the post-drift plateau is bounded by $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}/2$ precisely to
keep it from overlapping the adaptation it measures, and the bound is active at those
amplitudes (the window drops to $43$, $17$, $14$, $14$, $14$ steps respectively). The
symptom persists anyway. A negative $R$ means the mean error at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ sits
\emph{above} the plateau estimated just after the drift: the error is still rising when the
first tree is replaced. Calling the estimation artefact repaired would be false.

\paragraph{The plateau never reaches its theoretical value.} The control of the measured
plateau against $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ fails on all $20$ amplitudes, with a deviation
between $-0.0271$ and $-0.0080$, always negative. Two mechanisms account for it, and both
point at the baseline.

First, $\hat p_0$ is partly a product of the replacement mechanism. On the drift-free arm
of the campaign (100 runs, no change point at all), the forest still performs a median of
$14$ replacements and renews $9$ of its $10$ trees over the horizon, for a mean error of
$0.0226$. Replacement churn under pure noise is the normal regime. A direct comparison
against a forest that never replaces anything gave $0.0125$, but that figure is not
produced by this campaign: it comes from an exploratory measurement of 12 executions
(\texttt{resultats\_R2/JOURNAL.md} \S~2\,b) whose output was not persisted, and it is
quoted as an order of magnitude. Establishing the permanent cost of the replacement
mechanism properly is an open item of that journal.

Second, at large amplitudes the post-drift task is easier than the original one, so the
error does not settle at $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ at all. The stylised model of the subject
fails at the top of the grid, and that is a measurement rather than a conjecture.

\section{An Observation on the Race}

Figure~\ref{fig:joint} makes visible something the aggregate framing of the paradox hides.
At $\lambda = 8$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ in $95.44\%$ of the $1\,906$
uncensored runs, or $90.95\%$ of all $2\,000$ runs when censored ones are imputed as
losses. At $\lambda = 25$ the proportion falls to $6.56\%$, and at $\lambda = 50$ it is
$0\%$ on the single uncensored run.

Those grid averages need one qualification, because the rate is far from stationary in
$\Delta e$. At $\lambda = 8$ the detector wins $0$ of the $10$ uncensored runs at
$\Delta e = 0.028$ (where $90\%$ of runs never alarm at all), $62\%$ at
$\Delta e = 0.085$, and between $88\%$ and $99\%$ everywhere above. The $95.44\%$ figure
is dominated by the 18 easy amplitudes out of 20. The blind spot is therefore a property
of the pair $(\lambda, \Delta e)$ rather than of $\lambda$ alone: even at the most
sensitive threshold tested, it is fully present at the bottom of the grid.

\section{Not Addressed}

The official statement of question~D asks for the correlation of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$
with the residual error and with the effective time of return to normal. The second is not
computed as a per-run correlation anywhere in Q~D, and the reason belongs here rather than
in silence.

$\tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ is defined only on the seed-averaged error curve, one value per
amplitude: on a single run the binary sequence is far too noisy for a threshold crossing to
signal genuine recovery. The correlations of Q~D are computed run by run, so the two
objects are not commensurable. It appears in Figure~\ref{fig:joint} as the
$\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ marker, the only use its definition supports, dashed at the
amplitudes where the test has no power to resolve it.

``Return of the error to an acceptable level'' also fails as an adaptation criterion, for a
reason established elsewhere in this work. At $\Delta e = 0.498$ the threshold
$\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$ is crossed at step $46$, with or without the persistence
requirement, because the post-drift problem is easier than the original one and the error
falls below the pre-drift baseline. A criterion that a simplified problem satisfies
immediately does not measure adaptation.

Finally, the campaign exists at $M = 10$ only, so no statement in Q~D holds as a function
of forest size. The instrumented runs at other values of $M$ were produced for a different
test and carry no evidence trace.
