\noindent All numerical measurements in this section come from the instrumented
campaign of 2\,000 runs (20 amplitudes $\times$ 100 seeds, $H=2\,000$, $M=10$ trees)
documented in \texttt{04\_experimentations/resultats\_R2/}. Every figure quoted below is
reproduced by \texttt{scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} and \texttt{scripts/derives\_QD.py}. The
deterministic hierarchy $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$ (Q~C.1) is
assumed established.

\section{What Is Recorded and What Is Recomputed}

The simulation writes raw material only: the error trajectory $e_t \in \{0,1\}$, the tree
replacement events, and the $3\,000$ pre-drift steps. No threshold, no indicator, no
$\lambda$ appears anywhere in the simulation loop.

This is a design decision, not a convenience. The external detector reads the error
trajectory and never acts on the forest: $\lambda$ has no influence whatsoever on the
dynamics. Once the \texttt{break} is removed, the three scenarios of R2 produce runs that
are identical to the bit. One campaign therefore replaces three, and $S_t$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$ are swept offline for all three thresholds
and for any $\delta_P$. Freezing a threshold inside the simulation loop would have cost
one campaign per threshold, and at $\lambda = 50$ it would have produced a table in which
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is censored in $99.95\%$ of runs --- a column on which no
correlation can be computed at all.

  \label{fig:joint}

\paragraph{No temporal smoothing.} $e_t$ is binary. The only averaging applied is the
inter-seed mean, taken step by step; no sliding filter is used anywhere. A moving average
of length $L$ shifts the curve by half its support and destroys the synchronisation with
$\tau^*$, which is precisely the quantity this figure exists to display. At the top of the
grid the median $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $28$ steps, so a $50$-step filter would displace
the curve by nearly the whole quantity being measured.

\paragraph{The four markers.} $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is plotted at $\lambda = 8$, the only
threshold at which censoring stays in the minority ($4.70\%$, against $61.10\%$ at
$\lambda = 25$ and $99.95\%$ at $\lambda = 50$). At the other two thresholds a median
delay would be computed on a minority of runs, and it is not published.
$\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is the return of the mean error below
$\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$ with $\rho = 0.25$, held for $20$ consecutive steps; it is the
quantity written $\tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ in Q~C.1, and the two names denote the same
object throughout. The
persistence requirement is not cosmetic: without it, two amplitudes show an apparent
crossing at the very first post-drift step, which is a fluctuation and not a recovery, and
two more cross at step $101$, well before the median $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, without the
crossing being sustained.

\paragraph{Replacement dynamics.} At $t = 100$ the median $N_t/M$ ranges from $0.00$ to
$0.70$ across amplitudes, with an interquartile range of $0.00$ to $0.30$; at $t = 400$,
from $0.10$ to $0.90$; at the end of the horizon, from $0.60$ to $1.00$. The proportion of
runs in which the whole forest has been renewed by $t = 1\,999$ runs from $2\%$ to $94\%$
depending on amplitude.

\section{The Baseline on Two Windows}

The baseline $\hat p_0$ governs $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$, hence
every censoring rate reported in Q~D.4. It is therefore reported on both windows, as the
protocol requires: $1\,000$ pre-drift steps (reproducing R2 as published) and $3\,000$
steps (used for the analysis). The $3\,000$ pre-drift steps recorded per run make this a
purely offline recomputation --- no simulation is replayed. As a control, the baseline
recomputed at $3\,000$ steps reproduces the campaign value with a maximum deviation of
exactly $0$.

The choice of window shifts the censoring rates by less than one point and changes no
verdict. It is reported because the protocol demands it, and because the direction of the
shift is instructive: a baseline estimated over $3\,000$ steps is slightly higher, so the
detector accumulates slightly less evidence and alarms slightly less often.

\paragraph{Comparison with the theoretical baseline.} The pre-drift target
$y = \mathbf{1}\{x_0 + x_1 > 0\}$ is a deterministic function of the inputs: there is no
label noise, and the Bayes error is exactly $0$. The measured baseline of $0.023$ is
therefore \emph{entirely} approximation error --- axis-parallel splits approximating an
oblique boundary. The detector monitors departures from a reference level which is an
artefact of the classifier, not a property of the problem.

\section{What Is Acquired at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$}

Two quantities are measured at the instant $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$: the fraction of
adaptation already acquired, $R(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$, and the fraction of evidence
already accumulated by the detector, $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$. $G$ is computed run by run,
$R$ per amplitude on the mean curve, and $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is taken as the inter-seed
median.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{resultats_R2/resultats/figures/Fig_QD_R_et_G_full.png}
  \caption{$R(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$ and $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$ against $\Delta e$.
  Neither is monotone, and $R$ is negative at five amplitudes.}
  \label{fig:rg}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Answer.} $G$ ranges from $0.4889$ to $0.7594$ over the 18 amplitudes of the
decision domain --- between half and three quarters of the evidence is already available
to the detector when the first tree is replaced. On the full grid it falls to $0.1023$,
and that restriction is published with the figure.

$R$, on the other hand, does not support a single sentence. It runs from $-0.1882$ to
$+0.8853$ on the grid and from $-0.1882$ to $+0.5883$ on the decision domain, and it is
\textbf{not monotone in $\Delta e$}. At $\Delta e = 0.085$ it reaches $0.885$: nearly all
of the adaptation is already acquired when the first tree is replaced, which is the
opposite of the reading one would expect. At $\Delta e = 0.141$ it is $0.588$.

The formula ``early in the adaptation, late in the race'' is therefore \textbf{not
supported by the table} and is not used here. What the measurement supports is narrower
and is stated as such: at the instant $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, between $49\%$ and $76\%$ of
the evidence is already accumulated, while the fraction of adaptation acquired varies from
negative to $89\%$ with no monotone dependence on amplitude.

\paragraph{A negative fraction of adaptation, and why it persists.} $R$ is negative at
five amplitudes ($\Delta e = 0.243$, $0.436$, $0.494$, $0.496$, $0.498$), reaching
$-0.1882$. The window used to estimate the post-drift plateau is bounded by
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}/2$ precisely to prevent it from overlapping the adaptation it
measures, and the bound \emph{is} active at those amplitudes (the window drops to $43$,
$17$, $14$, $14$, $14$ steps respectively). The symptom nonetheless persists. It is not an estimation artefact
that has been repaired: a negative $R$ means the mean error at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is
\emph{above} the plateau estimated just after the drift, i.e.\ the error is still rising
when the first tree is replaced. Reporting this as ``corrected'' would be false.

\paragraph{The plateau never reaches its theoretical value.} The control of the measured
plateau against $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ fails on $20$ amplitudes out of $20$, with a
deviation between $-0.0271$ and $-0.0080$ --- always negative, never once positive. The
measured plateau is systematically below the value the stylised model predicts. Two
mechanisms account for it, and both point at the baseline rather than at the plateau.

First, $\hat p_0$ is not a property of the problem but partly a product of the replacement
mechanism itself. On the drift-free arm of the campaign (100 runs, no change point at
all), the forest still performs a median of $14$ replacements and renews a median of
$9$ of its $10$ trees over the horizon, for a mean error of $0.0226$. Replacement churn
under pure noise is therefore the normal regime, not an exception. A direct comparison
against a forest that never replaces anything gave $0.0125$; that figure is \emph{not}
produced by this campaign. It comes from an exploratory measurement of 12 executions
(\texttt{resultats\_R2/JOURNAL.md} \S~2\,b) whose output was not persisted, and it is
quoted as an order of magnitude, not as a campaign result. Establishing the permanent cost
of the replacement mechanism properly is listed among the open items of that journal.

Second, at large amplitudes the post-drift task is \emph{easier} than the original one, so
the error does not settle at $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ at all. The stylised model of the
subject is in default at the top of the grid, and this is measured, not conjectured.

\section{An Observation on the Race Itself}

Figure~\ref{fig:joint} makes visible a fact that the aggregate framing of the paradox
hides. At $\lambda = 8$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ in $95.44\%$ of the
$1\,906$ uncensored runs: the detector wins the race almost always. At $\lambda = 25$ the
proportion falls to $6.56\%$, and at $\lambda = 50$ it is $0\%$ (on the single uncensored
run). The blind spot is therefore not a property of the apparatus but of the threshold
setting, and the transition between the two regimes is sharp.

\section{Not Addressed}

The official statement of question~D asks for the correlation of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$
with the residual error \emph{and with the effective time of return to normal}. The
second of these is not computed as a per-run correlation anywhere in Q~D, and the reason
is stated here rather than left implicit.

$\tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ is defined only on the seed-averaged error curve, one value
per amplitude: on a single run the binary sequence is far too noisy for a threshold
crossing to signal genuine recovery. The correlations of Q~D are computed run by run, so
the two objects are not commensurable. It appears in Figure~\ref{fig:joint} as the
$\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ marker, which is the only use its definition supports, and it is
dashed there at the amplitudes where the test has no power to resolve it.

Independently of that, ``return of the error to an acceptable level'' fails as an
adaptation criterion for a reason established elsewhere in this work: at
$\Delta e = 0.498$ the threshold $\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$ is crossed at step $46$, with
or without the persistence requirement, not because the forest has understood anything but
because the post-drift problem is easier than the original one and the error falls
\emph{below} the pre-drift baseline. A criterion that a simplified problem satisfies
immediately does not measure adaptation.

Also not addressed: the campaign exists at $M = 10$ only, so no statement in Q~D is
established as a function of forest size. The instrumented runs at other values of $M$
were produced for a different test and carry no evidence trace.
