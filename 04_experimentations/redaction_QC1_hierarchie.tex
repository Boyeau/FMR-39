\noindent All numerical measurements in this section come from the instrumented
campaign of 2\,000 runs (20 amplitudes $\times$ 100 seeds, $H=2\,000$, baseline
$\hat p_0$ estimated over the last 3\,000 pre-drift steps) documented in
\texttt{04\_experimentations/resultats\_R2/}.

\section{Deterministic Relation Between $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$}

Let $N_t$ be the number of distinct trees replaced at time $t$ since the drift
$\tau^*$, and
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q) = \inf\{t > \tau^* : N_t/M \ge q\} - \tau^*.
\]
$N_t$ can only increase (a replaced tree stays counted), so $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}$ is
increasing in $q$. And since $N_t$ is an integer, the condition $N_t \ge 1$ is
equivalent to $N_t/M \ge 1/M$~: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is therefore exactly
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(1/M)$, i.e.\ $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(0.10)$ for $M=10$. Hence, for
every $q \ge 1/M$~:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)} \qquad \text{on every run,
  with no probabilistic assumption.}
  \label{eq:relation}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Empirical verification.} Across the campaign's 2\,000 runs,
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(10\%)$ exactly in 100\% of cases (0 gap).
Inequality~\eqref{eq:relation} holds without exception across the 5\,795 uncensored
comparisons available for $q \in \{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$ (0 violations).

\paragraph{Direction of the bias.} Since inequality~\eqref{eq:relation} is strict in
the vast majority of cases, using $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ as the forest's adaptation date
\emph{systematically and one-sidedly underestimates} the time needed for a genuinely
significant fraction of the ensemble to have been renewed~: this is a one-sided,
optimistic bias, never offset by a symmetric overestimate.

\paragraph{Relation to the theoretical side (Q~B.2) --- two distinct effects, not one.}
It is tempting to read this result as a confirmation of Q~B.2
(\texttt{redaction\_QB\_biais\_independance.tex}), but the two should not be
conflated. Inequality~\eqref{eq:relation} is a purely combinatorial fact about the
counting process $N_t$~: it holds pathwise, on every single run, regardless of any
dependence structure between trees --- it would hold identically if the trees were
independent, positively dependent, or anything else. Q~B.2, by contrast, is a
statement about model misspecification~: it compares two different probability laws
for $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself (the article's independence assumption versus the
true, positively-dependent law), and says nothing about $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$.
The two results are independent and \emph{compounding}, not the same fact observed
twice~: even with perfect knowledge of the true dependent law of
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, it would still underestimate real forest-wide recovery (this
section) --- and, separately, the article's independence assumption additionally
underestimates $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself (Q~B.2). Both push in the same practical
direction, for unrelated reasons.

\section{Gap $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q) - \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ as a Function of $\Delta e$}

Inequality~\eqref{eq:relation} only tells us the sign of the gap, not its magnitude.
Figure~\ref{fig:ecarts} (full campaign, inter-seed median) plots
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q) - \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ as a function of $\Delta e$, for
$q \in \{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$ ($q=10\%$ is omitted~: the gap there is identically zero,
since $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(0.10)$).

\paragraph{Substitutability criterion.} Fixed before reading the final results~: a gap
exceeding 100 steps ($5\%$ of the horizon $H=2\,000$) is deemed disqualifying for
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ as a substitute for $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$.
%
% Methodological note: Ulysse's folder proposes a second criterion -- the gap is
% deemed decisive if it exceeds tau_det -- more directly tied to the object of the
% study, but formulated after reading the measurements (flag as such if reused,
% cf. his reservation C.1b in resultats_R2/README.md).

With this criterion, on the full grid~: for $q=25\%$, the gap falls below 100 steps as
soon as $\Delta e \gtrsim 0.24$ (median gap of 291 at $\Delta e=0.028$, versus
6.5--7 steps beyond $\Delta e=0.48$) --- $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ becomes an acceptable
substitute again there. For $q=50\%$, the same threshold is only crossed around
$\Delta e \gtrsim 0.33$ (gap of 633 at $\Delta e=0.028$, versus 18--19 steps at the top
of the grid). For $q=75\%$, the gap \emph{never} falls below 100 steps anywhere on the
tested grid (167 to 1\,393 steps depending on $\Delta e$, i.e.\ 6 to 15 times
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself)~: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is at no point an acceptable
proxy for ``three-quarters of the forest have been renewed''.

\section{The Conjecture $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$}

$e_t \in \{0,1\}$ is only statistically meaningful once averaged over seeds~: on a
single run, the sequence is too noisy for a threshold crossing to signal genuine
recovery. $\tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ is therefore computed only on the mean curve
$\bar e_t$, one value per amplitude, never per run --- and compared to the
\emph{inter-seed median} of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ (the only quantity comparable on a
run-by-run basis).

\paragraph{Conjecture.} Incremental learning by the non-replaced Hoeffding trees can
bring the ensemble error back down without any tree having changed, particularly at
low amplitudes where the shock is too faint to require a replacement. Under this
hypothesis, $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ would not hold at low
$\Delta e$.

\paragraph{Test and result.} With $\rho = 0.25$, a first threshold crossing
($\bar e_t - \hat p_0 \le \rho \Delta e$) with no persistence condition shows 2
apparent violations, at the two lowest amplitudes of the grid ($\Delta e = 0.028$ and
$0.085$). But the residual noise of the mean curve ($\sigma \approx 0.015$, estimated
over the last 500 steps) is of the same order of magnitude as the threshold
$\rho \Delta e$ itself ($\rho\Delta e = 0.007$ and $0.021$ respectively)~: the test has
statistically no power there ($\rho \Delta e < 2\sigma$ in both cases). Requiring the
condition to hold for 20 consecutive steps rather than one, both violations
disappear~: \textbf{0 violations across the 18 interpretable amplitudes, and the test
loses all statistical validity precisely at the 2 amplitudes where it indicated one.}

\paragraph{Conclusion.} The conjecture is not confirmed --- nor is it refuted at low
amplitudes, where the experimental setup lacks power. More seeds would be needed~:
since $\bar e_t$ is a mean of $n$ independent Bernoulli draws, the noise of the mean
curve decreases as $1/\sqrt{n}$, and reaching $\rho\Delta e > 2\sigma$ would require on
the order of $200$ seeds at $\Delta e = 0.085$, but nearly $1\,800$ at
$\Delta e = 0.028$ --- and more still in practice, since the precision of a crossing
instant also depends on the slope of the curve at the threshold, which is shallow
precisely at these amplitudes. Across the 18 amplitudes where the test is
interpretable, $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{err}}(\rho)$ holds without
exception~: the first tree replacement always precedes the actual recovery of the
error, consistent with the optimistic bias established in~\eqref{eq:relation}.
