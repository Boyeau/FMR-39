\section{The Two Units, and What a Direct Comparison Erases}

\noindent\emph{Question addressed.} What is the physical unit of
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, and that of the threshold $\lambda$? How does comparing them
directly sidestep the sequential nature of the detector?

\paragraph{The two units.} In a stream, there is no physical clock~: the only time
variable available is the index of the incoming example, so $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is
measured in \emph{observations}. The threshold, meanwhile, is read off the recurrence
of the cumulative detector\footnote{Equation~(3) of the manuscript, section~III-D
(``The Starvation Effect''), notation kept as is. The R2 protocol creates the detector
at the drift instant, hence $S_{\tau^*} = 0$~; the manuscript's Proposition~3 instead
keeps $S_{\tau^*} = s_0 \ge 0$ --- setting $s_0 = 0$ here is a specialization of the
protocol.}
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \max\bigl(0,\, S_{t-1} + e_t - p_0 - \delta_P\bigr)\,,
  \label{eq:cusum}
\end{equation}
where $e_t \in \{0,1\}$ flags an error at step $t$, $p_0$ the normal pre-drift error
rate, and $\delta_P$ a tolerance subtracted at every step. Since $e_t$ is $0$ or $1$,
an error pushes $S$ up by nearly one unit while a success barely brings it down~: up to
the tolerance, $S_t$ counts the errors accumulated beyond the baseline. Since
$\lambda$ only ever appears in the comparison $S_t \ge \lambda$, it must be read in
the same unit --- $\lambda = 50$ does not mean ``wait 50 steps'', but ``wait until
roughly 50 excess errors have accumulated''. The two quantities are therefore counts
of different natures~: writing $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < \lambda$ makes no dimensional
sense.

\paragraph{What a direct comparison hides.} Comparing the two amounts to treating
$\lambda$ as a duration~: ``it takes $\lambda$ errors, hence so many steps''. This
shortcut assumes evidence accumulates at a fixed rate --- without an error-per-step
rate, there is no way to convert one unit into the other --- and the assumption is
baked into the very act of comparing, whether or not the conversion is written out.
But no such rate exists~: the detector receives errors one at a time and does not
output a number, but a date --- the one at which the trajectory of $S_t$ crosses
$\lambda$. Three features of that trajectory disappear once it is replaced by a
constant-slope ramp.

\emph{The rate slows down.} Right after the drift, the counter's increment has
expectation $\Delta e - \delta_P$~; but the forest repairs itself, the excess error
drifts back toward the baseline, and this gain dries up. The manuscript instead
assumes a constant excess of $\Delta e$ throughout the whole transient, then none at
all --- a rectangle, whose height is precisely the fixed rate the comparison requires.

\emph{Acquired evidence can be destroyed.} When $S_t$ would go below zero, the
$\max(0,\cdot)$ in~\eqref{eq:cusum} resets it to $0$~: this is not a pause, everything
accumulated so far is lost. The same errors, clustered right after the drift or spread
out over the horizon, therefore do not yield the same alarm date, whereas an average
rate makes no distinction between the two. This is the precise meaning of
``sequential''~: the date depends on the order of arrival, not only on the total.

\emph{The alarm may never come.} Over the campaign's horizon $H = 2\,000$, the
threshold may never be crossed\footnote{Beyond the horizon the crossing eventually
happens, but its mean delay grows exponentially with $\lambda$ (manuscript,
Remark~4)~: the alarm then no longer bears on the drift.}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} An error count and an observation count cannot be compared by
positing a rate. Homogeneity is restored the other way around~: by bringing both
quantities into the unit of evidence, where the comparison is threshold against
accumulated evidence, on each run and without averaging. That is the object of the
peak statistic $S_{\max}(H)$ and the deterministic certificate that follows from it,
developed in Section~2 below.

\section{A Deterministic Certificate: The Peak Statistic}

\noindent\emph{Question addressed.} Show that the CUSUM's peak statistic can be
written $S_{\max}(H) = \max_{0 \le k \le j \le H}\bigl[A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P\bigr]$,
the maximum ranging over all sub-windows $]k,j] \subseteq [0,H]$.

\paragraph{Notation.} Post-drift steps are indexed $t = 1,\dots,H$, with $t=0$ the
drift instant itself. Following the statement, set
\[
  A(k,j) \;=\; \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} \bigl(e_t - \hat p_0\bigr),
  \qquad
  X_t \;=\; e_t - \hat p_0 - \delta_P,
  \qquad
  S_0 = 0,
  \qquad
  S_t = \max\bigl(0,\, S_{t-1} + X_t\bigr),
\]
and $S_{\max}(H) = \max_{0 \le t \le H} S_t$. The baseline here is the \emph{estimated}
baseline $\hat p_0$, as throughout Question~C. By convention an empty sum is $0$, so
$A(j,j) = 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Lindley's closed form]\label{prop:lindley}
For every $j \in \{0,\dots,H\}$,
\begin{equation}
  S_j \;=\; \max_{0 \le k \le j}\ \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t
  \;=\; \max_{0 \le k \le j}\ \bigl[A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P\bigr].
  \label{eq:lindley}
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum over $j$ gives
$S_{\max}(H) = \max_{0 \le k \le j \le H}\bigl[A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P\bigr]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $j$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Base case.} For $j = 0$, the only admissible index is $k = 0$ and the
corresponding sum is empty, hence zero~: the right-hand side equals $0 = S_0$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Inductive step.} Assume~\eqref{eq:lindley} holds at rank $j-1$. Then
\[
  S_j \;=\; \max\bigl(0,\; S_{j-1} + X_j\bigr)
      \;=\; \max\Bigl(0,\; \max_{0 \le k \le j-1} \sum_{t=k+1}^{j-1} X_t \;+\; X_j\Bigr)
      \;=\; \max\Bigl(0,\; \max_{0 \le k \le j-1} \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t\Bigr),
\]
the last equality following from $X_j$ not depending on $k$, so it may enter the
maximum. Now the term $0$ is exactly the empty sum obtained for $k = j$~: the two
arguments of the outer maximum merge into a single maximum over $0 \le k \le j$,
giving~\eqref{eq:lindley} at rank $j$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Conclusion.} The second form follows by expanding the sum~:
\[
  \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t
  \;=\; \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} \bigl(e_t - \hat p_0\bigr) \;-\; (j-k)\,\delta_P
  \;=\; A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P ,
\]
since $\delta_P$ is subtracted once per step and the sub-window $]k,j]$ contains
$j-k$ of them. Finally
$S_{\max}(H) = \max_{0 \le j \le H} S_j
 = \max_{0 \le j \le H} \max_{0 \le k \le j} [\,\cdot\,]
 = \max_{0 \le k \le j \le H} [\,\cdot\,]$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the identity makes visible.} The reset to zero is not a technical
correction~: it is \emph{equivalent} to the freedom to choose the starting point. A
CUSUM that restarts from zero whenever the counter would go below the line does
nothing other than retain, at every instant, the best recent sub-window. This is why
the maximum ranges over pairs $(k,j)$ rather than over $j$ alone~: the detector does
not measure the evidence accumulated since the drift, but the evidence accumulated
over the most favorable sub-window.

Two immediate consequences. First, $S_{\max}(H) \ge 0$, since the choice $k=j$ is
always available. Second, and more importantly, the identity is \emph{deterministic}~:
it holds on each individual run, with no averaging or distributional assumption. This
is what licenses the certificate used in Q~C.3.

\paragraph{Numerical check.} The closed form~\eqref{eq:lindley} was checked against
the naive recurrence over every trajectory of the campaign~: maximal deviation
$1.07 \times 10^{-10}$, i.e.\ machine precision. The analysis script refuses to
proceed further if this check fails.
