\noindent All numerical measurements in this section come from the instrumented
campaign of 2\,000 runs (20 amplitudes $\times$ 100 seeds, $H=2\,000$, baseline
$\hat p_0$ estimated over the last 3\,000 pre-drift steps) documented in
\texttt{04\_experimentations/resultats\_R2/}. The deterministic hierarchy
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$ (Q~C.1) and the dimensional analysis
with its detection certificate (Q~C.2) are assumed established.

\section{Budget Expression and Residual Exponent}

The article's stylized model assumes that after the drift the error exceeds its
baseline by an amount $\Delta e$ for a transient of length $W = \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$,
then falls back to baseline. The excess-error area offered to the detector is then
$\mathbb{E}[A] = \Delta e \cdot \mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}]$, provided the horizon
fully covers the transient, which $H = 2\,000$ guarantees across the whole grid ---
the lowest amplitude gives $3 \times 18.5/0.028 \approx 1\,982$. Plugging in the
article's empirical fit
$\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}] \approx 18.5\,\Delta e^{-0.98}$~:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\mathbb{E}[A] \approx 18.5\,\Delta e^{\,1-0.98} = 18.5\,\Delta e^{\,0.02}}
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Residual exponent and variation across the grid.} The residual exponent of
$\Delta e$ is $0.02$. Over $\Delta e \in [0.028\,;\,0.498]$, the ratio of the extremes
is $(0.498/0.028)^{0.02} = 1.059$~: the predicted budget varies by $5.9\%$, from
$17.2$ to $18.2$, as the shock amplitude varies by a factor of $17.7$.

\paragraph{Formal conclusion.} Amplitude and duration nearly exactly offset each
other~: the forest repairs itself all the faster the stronger the shock, and the
product $\Delta e \times W$ stays close to constant. A violent macroeconomic shock
therefore leaves the detector no more evidence than a slow drift. The budget behaves
like a constant of the forest-detector pair, not as a characteristic of the observed
drift.

\section{The Actually Usable Budget}

The detector does not consume the raw area. Its tolerance $\delta_P = 0.01$ is
subtracted at every step, so the budget actually accumulable is
$(\Delta e - \delta_P)\,W$ rather than $\Delta e\,W$. The confiscated share is
therefore $\delta_P/\Delta e$, independent of the transient's duration.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\Delta e$ & raw budget & share taken & usable budget \\
\midrule
$0.028$ (bottom of grid) & $17.23$ & $\mathbf{35.5\%}$ & $11.11$ \\
$0.498$ (top of grid) & $18.24$ & $2.0\%$ & $17.88$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Where the tolerance bites.} It is at the \emph{low} end of the grid that
$\delta_P$ takes a non-negligible share~: more than a third of the budget at
$\Delta e = 0.028$, versus $2\%$ at the other end. Since the tolerance is an absolute
threshold, it weighs all the more heavily the weaker the signal. It therefore
\emph{worsens} the invariance rather than correcting it~: the usable budget is no
longer constant, it decreases toward low amplitudes --- exactly where the detector
would need it most.

\section{Confrontation with the Full-Window Certificate}

The certificate established in Q~C.2 requires, over the full window, a raw area of at
least $\lambda + H\delta_P$. With $H = 2\,000$ and $\delta_P = 0.01$ the toll is $20$
regardless of the threshold, giving $28$, $45$ and $70$ for
$\lambda \in \{8, 25, 50\}$, to be compared to the budget of about $18$ established
in~\eqref{eq:budget}. \textbf{None of the three thresholds is reachable}, the lowest
already requiring one and a half times the available budget. The required evidence
does not exist in the signal, whatever the setting chosen.

\paragraph{What happens to the certificate as the horizon lengthens.} The required
threshold $\lambda + H\delta_P$ grows \emph{linearly} in $H$, while the budget stays
\emph{constant} per~\eqref{eq:budget}. The gap therefore widens indefinitely~: once
the error has recovered, every additional observation step adds $\delta_P$ to the toll
without contributing anything to the numerator, so that observing longer degrades the
certificate instead of improving it. Hence the value of anchoring it on a short window
$w = 3 \times 18.5/\Delta e$, sized to the transient of the amplitude under
consideration.

\section{Verification Against the Measurements}

  compared to the constant predicted by~\eqref{eq:budget}. In blue the area over the
  short window $A(w)$, in red the area over the full horizon $A(H)$.}
  \label{fig:budget}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The invariance holds in sign, not in value.} The lowest amplitude
($\Delta e = 0.028$) is deliberately left out of the comparison below~: it is one of
the two amplitudes already flagged in Q~C.1 as statistically non-interpretable for the
recovery test, because the shock there is smaller than the noise floor of the mean
error curve. Its measured area ($2.71$) is correspondingly tiny --- not because the
compensation mechanism breaks down, but because there is barely a signal to
accumulate in the first place. Including it would inflate the apparent range to a
factor of $11$ (from $2.71$ to $30.8$), which would be a noise-floor artifact rather
than evidence against the invariance.

Over $\Delta e \in [0.085\,;\,0.498]$, the shock amplitude varies by a factor of
$5.8$, and absent forest adaptation the area would vary by as much. It actually
varies by a factor of $\mathbf{1.78}$~: the compensating effect is indeed present.
But the model predicts a factor of $1.04$, and it underestimates the budget in the
middle of the grid, with $30.8$ measured against $18.0$ predicted at
$\Delta e = 0.243$. The invariance is thus confirmed in principle and approximate in
its values.

\paragraph{An earlier warning sign.} The ratio $A(w)/A(H)$ exposes the same problem
well before $A(H)$ itself turns negative. It stays below $1$ across the first half of
the grid ($0.80$ to $0.93$)~: $A(H)$ still captures a little more than $A(w)$, coming
from the mildly positive tail of the transient beyond $w$. But it crosses $1$ around
$\Delta e \approx 0.39$ --- past this point, whatever happens between $w$ and $H$ is
already working, on net, against the full-window measurement, well before it collapses
into negative territory at $\Delta e \approx 0.48$ (see below). The short window is
therefore not merely a fix for the extreme case~: it stops relying on a portion of the
horizon that turns counterproductive much earlier than where the full-window area
actually goes negative.

\paragraph{Why the lowest amplitude is set aside, and what it costs.} The factor of
$1.78$ is computed over $[0.085\,;\,0.498]$, not over the full grid. Including
$\Delta e = 0.028$, where $A(w)$ collapses to $2.71$, the measured area varies by a factor
of $\mathbf{11.4}$ instead, and the invariance no longer holds in any useful sense.
The exclusion is not a matter of convenience~: at that amplitude the short window
$w = 1\,969$ steps nearly covers the whole horizon, so $A(w)$ and $A(H)$ stop being
distinguishable and the short-window construction loses its meaning. But the restriction
must be stated with the result, since it is what separates a factor of $1.8$ from a factor
of $11$.

\paragraph{The full-window area turns negative.} $A(H)$ peaks at $38.3$ then
decreases, crosses zero from $\Delta e = 0.482$ onward and drops to $-20.8$. The
explanation lies in the geometry of the problem rather than in the measurement. At
large amplitude the post-drift boundary is $x_0 + x_1 > 4$ with
$x_0 + x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,2)$~: class 1 becomes nearly absent and the post-drift
task turns out to be easier than the original one. The measured error falls from its
$0.0231$ baseline to roughly $0.007$ across this high-amplitude region (e.g.\
$0.0071$ at $\Delta e = 0.49$), and this deficit subtracts about thirty units from the
raw area over the $1\,900$ steps following recovery. The stylized model is therefore at fault at large amplitude, where the error
drops below its initial baseline instead of returning to it. This is a second reason,
independent of the one given in Q~C.2, to certify only on the short window.

\paragraph{Where does the gap come from~: the baseline, the censoring, or the fit?}
The subject invites us to look first at $\hat p_0$. Its estimation error propagates in
a perfectly correlated way across the whole area, with a bias $-H(\hat p_0 - p_0)$~;
estimated over 3\,000 steps, $\hat p_0$ has an inter-seed standard deviation of
$0.00298$, i.e.\ $\pm 6.0$ on $A(H)$. This is non-negligible against a budget of $18$
--- but insufficient to account for the gap of $14.5$ observed at $\Delta e = 0.028$,
especially since this bias has a random sign while the observed gap is systematic.

The test that truly settles it is to compare the measured $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ to its
own prediction, since~\eqref{eq:budget} stacks two distinct hypotheses~: the model
$\mathbb{E}[A] = \Delta e\,\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}]$ on one hand, and the
power-law fit on the other.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
$\Delta e$ & measured $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ & predicted & ratio & $\Delta e \times \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ & measured $A(w)$ \\
\midrule
$0.028$ & $118$ & $611$ & $\mathbf{0.19}$ & $3.33$ & $2.71$ \\
$0.141$ & $263$ & $126$ & $\mathbf{2.08}$ & $37.07$ & $27.09$ \\
$0.287$ & $64$ & $63$ & $1.02$ & $18.37$ & $29.22$ \\
$0.436$ & $34$ & $42$ & $0.81$ & $14.82$ & $23.16$ \\
$0.498$ & $28$ & $37$ & $0.76$ & $13.93$ & $17.31$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At the lowest amplitude, the fit predicts the first replacement at step $611$ whereas
it occurs at step $118$, five times earlier. The power-law fit is therefore at fault, and
the gap is not localized~: the measured-over-predicted ratio is $0.19$ at the bottom of the
grid, exceeds $2$ around $\Delta e = 0.141$, only matches around $\Delta e = 0.287$, then
falls back to $0.76$ at the top.

\paragraph{But the underlying model fails too.} It would be convenient to conclude that
only the fit is at fault and that the model
$\mathbb{E}[A] = \Delta e\,\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}]$ survives. The measurements do
not support this. Recomputing $\Delta e \times \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ from the
\emph{measured} $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ at every amplitude and comparing it to the measured
$A(w)$, the model overestimates the area by $23\%$ to $43\%$ below
$\Delta e \approx 0.19$, and underestimates it by $20\%$ to $41\%$ above. It changes sign
around $\Delta e = 0.19$ and coincides with the measurement only there. At
$\Delta e = 0.028$ it gives $3.33$ against $2.71$ measured, and at $\Delta e = 0.287$,
$18.4$ against $29.2$.

The reason is the one Q~C.1 establishes: $W = \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is not the duration of
the transient. $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ dates the \emph{first} tree replacement, whereas
three-quarters of the forest is renewed $6$ to $14$ times later. Taking the first
replacement as the width of the error plateau is inconsistent with the hierarchy proved in
Q~C.1, and the sign reversal at $\Delta e \approx 0.19$ is the visible trace of that
inconsistency. Both hypotheses stacked in~\eqref{eq:budget} therefore fail, each in its
own range.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{\textbf{Consequence for the report.} The budget's
invariance rests on two successive hypotheses, and \emph{neither} survives the
measurements. The model $\mathbb{E}[A] = \Delta e \cdot \mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}]$
misses the measured area by $-41\%$ to $+43\%$ and reverses sign around
$\Delta e = 0.19$, because $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is not the width of the transient. The
power-law fit $18.5\,\Delta e^{-0.98}$ only holds near the middle of the grid. The
conclusion ``a violent shock leaves no more evidence than a slow drift'' is nonetheless
\emph{confirmed}, but by direct measurement alone~: the measured area varies by a factor
of $1.8$ where the amplitude varies by $5.8$. It can no longer be derived from the $0.02$
exponent, nor from the stylized rectangle. Both are scaffolding that the measurement has
to replace rather than support.}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The theoretical baseline.} The pre-drift target
$y = \mathbf{1}\{x_0 + x_1 > 0\}$ is a \emph{deterministic} function of the inputs,
with no label noise~: the Bayes error is exactly zero. The measured baseline,
$0.0231$, is therefore entirely approximation error --- Hoeffding trees cutting an
oblique boundary with axis-perpendicular splits. The detector thus monitors deviations
from a reference level that depends on the classifier itself~: a better-fitted forest
would lower the baseline and change the evidence budget with no property of the drift
having changed.

\section{What This Section Establishes}

The evidence budget offered to the detector is nearly independent of the drift's
amplitude~: the area varies by a factor of $1.8$ as the amplitude varies by a factor
of $5.8$, where it would track the latter absent adaptation. The tolerance
$\delta_P$ amputates this budget at low amplitudes --- and lengthening the observation
horizon widens the deficit further.

Three caveats accompany this result. The power-law fit on which the article bases the
invariance is only valid near the middle of the grid, so the argument must rest on direct
measurement rather than on the $0.02$ exponent. The stylized rectangle
$A = \Delta e \times \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is itself wrong, by $-41\%$ to $+43\%$ with a
sign reversal, because $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ dates the first replacement and not the end
of the transient. And the model is at fault at large amplitude for a third, independent
reason~: the error drops below its initial baseline instead of returning to it. The
invariance reported here is an empirical fact about the measured area, established
directly and stated over $\Delta e \in [0.085\,;\,0.498]$~; it is not a consequence of
the article's derivation.
