\noindent Every coefficient here is Kendall's $\tau_b$ with censored runs imputed to the
last rank, justified in Q~D.2. Every confidence interval (CI) is a bootstrap interval over
the seeds of the stratum, $2\,000$ resamples, quantiles $2.5\%$ and $97.5\%$. Values are produced by
\texttt{scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} from the campaign of 2\,000 runs (20 amplitudes $\times$
100 seeds, $H = 2\,000$, $M = 10$ trees in the adaptive random forest, ARF).

\section{Why Stratify}

$\Delta e$ drives both sides of every correlation of interest: it shortens
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and it raises the evidence available to the detector. A coefficient
computed on the pooled $2\,000$ runs therefore measures the common dependence on
$\Delta e$ rather than any association between the two quantities at a fixed operating
point. Stratification removes that common cause. Each stratum holds $100$ seeds, and the
amplitude is constant inside it by construction.

For $A(H)$ the pooled coefficient is $+0.3965$ against a median stratified coefficient of
$+0.0526$, a factor of $7.5$. This is not a strong association weakened by stratification:
the stratified coefficient is indistinguishable from zero under this protocol at every one
of the 18 amplitudes. The pooled coefficient manufactures a link rather than confirming a
weak one. Mechanically it is Simpson's paradox on $\Delta e$: within each stratum there is
nothing, and across strata both quantities move with the amplitude, so pooling recovers the
amplitude and reports it as an association.

The effect is strongest exactly where the within-stratum association is weakest: ratio
$7.54$ for $A(H)$ and $4.72$ for $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$, but only $1.48$ for
$S_{\max}(H)$, which does carry a genuine within-stratum signal. That is the diagnostic one
should expect, and it is why no pooled coefficient is quoted as a result in this work.

\section{A Tautological Row, Marked as Such}
\label{sec:tauto}

At $M = 10$ a quota of $10\%$ requires exactly one tree, so
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(10\%) = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(1/M) = \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ by
definition (Q~C.1a). Its $\tau_b$ against $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $1.000000$ at all
$20$ amplitudes with confidence interval $[1.000 ; 1.000]$, by identity rather than by
performance. The row is kept in Table~\ref{tab:agregation} and hatched in
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} rather than deleted, so the reader can check that the
instrumentation reproduces an identity it should reproduce. It carries no evidence about
anything else. The precedent is the treatment of $S_{\max}(H)$ under test B2 elsewhere in
this work, whose area under the ROC curve (AUC) of $1.000$ is tautological in the same
sense.

A related caution applies to the other quotas. At $M = 10$ neither $q = 25\%$ nor
$q = 75\%$ is exactly attainable: $25\%$ requires $3$ trees, that is $30\%$, and $75\%$
requires $8$, that is $80\%$. The labels are kept for continuity with Q~C.1, which uses the
same convention, but they name the attainable quota above the nominal one.

\section{The Central Result of Q~D}

The two quantities in Table~\ref{tab:strat} play different roles, and conflating them
changes the answer.

$A(H)$ is the accumulated excess error over the horizon. Its association with
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is indistinguishable from zero under this protocol at all
18 amplitudes: median $+0.0526$, every confidence interval straddling zero.

$S_{\max}(H)$ is the maximum of the path of the cumulative-sum (CUSUM) detector of Page,
$S_t = \max(0, S_{t-1} + e_t - \hat p_0 - \delta_P)$. Its association is discernible at 15 of the
18 amplitudes, with a median of $+0.2129$, and the confidence interval lies entirely above
zero at every amplitude from $\Delta e = 0.287$ upwards. The count is that of the
confidence-interval rule adopted in Q~D.4; the threshold rule of the \texttt{discernable}
column gives $14$, differing on the single cell $\Delta e = 0.287$.

The distinction matters because it is $S_{\max}(H)$ that decides the alarm, not $A(H)$. The
alarm is by definition the crossing of $\lambda$ by $S_{\max}$. A claim that
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ carries no information about the evidence offered to the detector is
attached to the wrong object if it rests on $A(H)$ alone. The answer, stated sharply:

\begin{quote}
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ carries \textbf{no information} about the total accumulated excess
error $A(H)$, indistinguishable from zero at all 18 amplitudes. It carries a \textbf{weak
but real} association with $S_{\max}(H)$, the quantity that determines whether the alarm
fires: median $\tau_b = +0.2129$, discernible at 15 of 18 amplitudes, that is every
amplitude from $\Delta e = 0.287$ upwards without exception. Below $\Delta e = 0.287$ the
association is not distinguishable from zero either.
\end{quote}

Read with Q~D.2, the direction of the residual bias reinforces this. $A(H)$ and
$S_{\max}(H)$ are never censored and carry no tie blocks, so the tie correction applies on
the $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ side only and displaces both coefficients towards zero. The
$A(H)$ result is thus not an artefact of ties inflating a null, and the $S_{\max}(H)$
result is if anything understated.

\section{Reservations}

\paragraph{Multiplicity.} The decision domain contains $108$ cells, of which $68$ are
significant at the $5\%$ level with no correction for multiplicity. The signal for
$S_{\max}(H)$ above $\Delta e = 0.287$ is far too systematic to be a multiplicity artefact,
running to $15$ consecutive amplitudes in the same direction (Figure~\ref{fig:forest}), but individual cells whose
$|\tau_b|$ is near the detectability threshold are not quoted on their own.

\paragraph{Detectability threshold.} The threshold used by the \texttt{discernable} column
is $0.13295637$, a single value across the table. It is quoted exactly rather than rounded,
since a rounded threshold would not reproduce the column. Q~D.4 examines the three cells
where the two rules disagree and fixes which one governs.

\paragraph{Scope.} Everything above is measured at $M = 10$ on one campaign. The two
amplitudes below $\Delta e = 0.10$ fall outside the decision domain and are excluded from
the medians quoted here, though they appear in Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}.
