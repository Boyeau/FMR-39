\section*{Context and Scope}

\paragraph*{The setting} A classifier deployed on a data stream can see the relation
between inputs and label change while the inputs themselves keep the same law: $P(Y \mid X)$
moves, $P(X)$ does not~\cite{moreno2012}. Such a concept drift is detected by watching the
classifier's error~\cite{gama2014,lu2018}. The Adaptive Random Forest (ARF)~\cite{gomes2017}
also repairs itself: each of its $M$ Hoeffding trees~\cite{domingos2000}, trained by online
bagging~\cite{oza2001}, watches its own error with an ADWIN detector~\cite{bifet2007} and is
replaced when it degrades. An external cumulative detector, a Page--Hinkley
test~\cite{page1954,hinkley1971} with threshold $\lambda$, watches the error of the whole
forest and is meant to tell the operator that the model has changed.

\paragraph*{The manuscript} The manuscript under study~\cite{blindspot2026} reports a
\emph{blind spot paradox} on River~\cite{montiel2021}: the stronger the drift, the faster
the forest repairs itself and the less evidence it leaves to the external detector, which
then stays silent or alarms after the repair. It dates the repair at
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, the first tree replacement, and compares this random time with a
nominal accumulation time $\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}} = \lambda/(\Delta e - \delta_P)$.

\paragraph*{The two corrections this work addresses} Two of the corrections requested by
the reviewers are the starting point of this work. First, the manuscript compares a random variable
with a constant, and assumes independence between trees without proof.
Second, it dates the adaptation of the forest at the first replaced tree without showing
that this instant has anything to do with the disappearance of the signal.

\paragraph*{What we find} Both corrections lead to the same reading: \textbf{the blind
spot is not a race between two clocks, but a budget of evidence set against a threshold.}
On every run, the detector arrives first if and only if its statistic reaches $\lambda$
before the first replacement (\hyperref[prop:race]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:race}}). Four messages structure the
report.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{M1 --- The race is well posed, and the manuscript is optimistic.} The miss
    event is defined without ad hoc rules, its probability is identified on the campaign,
    and the independence assumption provably overstates the speed of the repair
    (\hyperref[sec:A]{Sections~\ref*{sec:A}} and~\ref{sec:B}).
  \item \textbf{M2 --- The first replacement measures the repair poorly.} It is a lower
    bound by construction, it fires as fast without drift as on the weakest drift, and between
    $49\%$ and $76\%$ of the evidence is already accumulated when it occurs
    (\hyperref[sec:repair-date]{Section~\ref*{sec:repair-date}}).
  \item \textbf{M3 --- The alarm depends on evidence, not on time.} The alarm is a
    deterministic function of the error path, and the evidence available before the repair,
    between $13.1$ and $19.8$ in median above $\Delta e = 0.10$, sets the transition
    threshold and explains the paradoxical signature (\hyperref[sec:certificate]{Sections~\ref*{sec:certificate}}
    and~\ref{sec:E}).
  \item \textbf{M4 --- Detecting is not winning.} A threshold below that level wins the race;
    a threshold that detects almost every drift may still arrive after the repair
    (\hyperref[sec:E]{Section~\ref*{sec:E}}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The Race Between Two Clocks}
\label{sec:A}
% =====================================================================

The manuscript defines the blind spot as $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < \tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, then
replaces the random alarm time by the constant $\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}}$ and sets aside the
runs in which the detector never alarms. The race is a competing-risks problem observed
under censoring at $H$~\cite{fine1999,aalen1978}. This section defines the miss event on
the complete race, shows that its probability is identified on our campaign, and bounds it
from the marginal laws alone.

\subsection{Defining the Miss Event}
\label{sec:miss}

Everything that follows is a statement about $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$, and none of it means
anything until $\mathrm{Miss}$ is unambiguous. The natural reading --- the forest repairs
itself before the alarm sounds --- leaves three cases open: the two clocks coincide, the
detector never fires, or neither mechanism ever acts. We settle them by one definition
rather than three rules.

\paragraph{A strict inequality} Both clocks count integer steps, so they can land on
the same one and $P(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{det}})$ can be strictly positive:
the choice is not cosmetic, and no continuity argument disposes of it --- real-valued
times can coincide too, all the more so for two functionals of the same error stream.
It must therefore be settled on physical grounds. A tie means the alarm sounds
\emph{exactly} when the forest repairs itself: the operator is informed at that instant
and nothing is lost, unlike the blind spot proper where the alarm comes late or never. A
tie is a detection success, whence
\[
  \mathrm{Miss} \;:=\; \{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < \tau_{\mathrm{det}}\}
\]
on the extended reals, with strict inequality.
The convention carries almost no weight in practice: ties occur $0$ times out of
$1\,906$ observed races at $\lambda = 8$, $6$ out of $778$ at $\lambda = 25$, $0$ out of
$1$ at $\lambda = 50$, so the non-strict reading would move $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ by at
most $0.003$. They are rare not for any reason of continuity but because the two
mechanisms operate on visibly different time scales --- which is the substance of the
paradox rather than a technicality.

\paragraph{A silent repair is a miss} On
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < +\infty,\ \tau_{\mathrm{det}} = +\infty\}$ the forest repaired
itself and the alarm never sounded --- the blind spot in its purest form, worse than
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < \tau_{\mathrm{det}} < +\infty$ where the operator is at least
informed late. A finite $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is smaller than $+\infty$, so the event
falls inside $\mathrm{Miss}$ on its own.

It must. Excluding it would restrict the count to runs where the detector eventually
spoke --- the very selection bias this section holds against the manuscript, since those
discarded are the runs where it failed most completely. At $\lambda = 50$, where $1\,999$
runs out of $2\,000$ never fire, the rate would be computed over a single run.

\paragraph{A double starvation is not} On
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = +\infty,\ \tau_{\mathrm{det}} = +\infty\}$ neither mechanism
ever acted. The paradox rests on a \emph{silent repair} erasing the evidence; if the
repair never happened there is nothing to erase, and the model simply stays broken. Since
$\infty < \infty$ is false, the event is excluded on its own. Its empirical frequency is
$0$ at all three thresholds, so the convention is never exercised on this grid --- but
nothing in the model forbids it, and a slower forest would produce it.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{Adopted convention.}
\[ \mathrm{Miss} := \{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} < \tau_{\mathrm{det}}\} \qquad \text{(strict, extended reals)} \]
A tie goes to the detector, a silent repair is a miss, a double starvation is not --- all
three from the definition, none from a rule of its own. That is what makes it
trustworthy rather than fitted to the answer.}}
\end{center}

\subsection{Making the Miss Probability Observable}

$\mathrm{Miss}$ is defined on the complete race, with no time limit; the campaign
observes $H = 2\,000$ steps. On runs where neither clock has fired by then the winner is
not known, and $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ depends on what was never seen: it is not, as it
stands, computable from the data. Two ways out are unacceptable --- assuming that
censored runs behave like the others cannot be checked, and discarding them is the
selection bias just rejected. The aim is therefore to replace $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ by the
probability of an event that \emph{is} observable, or failing that to trap it between two
such probabilities.

\paragraph{What the horizon lets us see} Absolute censoring is
$C_H := \{\min(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}},\tau_{\mathrm{det}}) > H\}$, on which the race is
undecided. Sorting runs by the two observable events $\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le H\}$ and
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{det}} \le H\}$ gives a partition where the verdict is known everywhere
but one cell: regions~1 ($\le H$, $\le H$, decided directly) and~2 ($\le H$, $>H$, a
certain Miss) together form exactly $\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le H\} \cap \mathrm{Miss}$;
region~3 ($>H$, $\le H$) is never a Miss; region~4 is $C_H$, undetermined.

\paragraph{Trapping the unknown between two computable numbers} Region~4 can shift the
total by its own mass and no more. Write
$L := P(\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le H\} \cap \mathrm{Miss})$, computable from the data with
no assumption about what happens beyond $H$. Counting region~4 as never a Miss gives the
lower bound, entirely as Miss the upper one:
\[
  L \;\le\; P_{\mathrm{miss}} \;\le\; L + P(C_H).
\]
This is a Manski-type bound~\cite{manski2003}: nothing is assumed about the unobserved
runs, their possible influence is bounded by how many there are, and the width of the interval is exactly
$P(C_H)$ --- the extent of our ignorance.

\paragraph{On this campaign, the interval collapses} $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is
\emph{never} censored: $0$ runs out of $2\,000$, the largest value observed being
$1\,293$ steps. The forest, at maximal reactivity ($c_{\mathrm{int}} = 1$), always
replaces a tree before the horizon, so $\min(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}},\tau_{\mathrm{det}})$
is always finite, $C_H = \varnothing$, and the two bounds coincide:
\[
  P_{\mathrm{miss}} \;=\; P(\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le H\} \cap \mathrm{Miss}).
\]

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{Result.} The unconditional miss probability equals
the probability of an event observable within the horizon. This is an equality, not an
assumption: the naive computation would have returned the same number, but nothing would
have told us it was right. What the construction adds is the guarantee --- and the
condition under which it would fail. Were the forest able to stay inert past $H$,
$P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ would cease to be identified and only the interval would survive.}}
\end{center}

\subsection{Universal Fréchet Bound}

We now drop the finite horizon and ask what the two marginal laws concede on their own,
with no assumption on the coupling. This is a different kind of ignorance from the one
just treated: there, data were missing; here, a structure is.

\begin{proposition}[Fréchet bounds~\cite{frechet1951}]\label{prop:frechet}
For integer-valued $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, the latter possibly infinite,
with marginal CDFs $F_A$ and $F_D$, and for any coupling of the two,
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{miss}} &\ge \max\Big(0,\ \sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}} \big[F_A(s)-F_D(s)\big]\Big),\\
  P_{\mathrm{miss}} &\le \min\Big(1,\ \inf_{s\in\mathbb{N}} \big[F_A(s) + 1 - F_D(s+1)\big]\Big).
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\noindent The lower bound applies Fréchet's inequality to
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le s < \tau_{\mathrm{det}}\} \subseteq \mathrm{Miss}$; the upper bound
follows from $\mathrm{Miss} \subseteq \{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le s\} \cup
\{\tau_{\mathrm{det}} > s+1\}$ and Boole's inequality. Both proofs, and a toy case on which
both bounds are attained, are in \hyperref[app:proofs]{Appendix~\ref*{app:proofs}}.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{Result.} The two bounds above hold for
\emph{any} dependence structure between $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$: they use the marginal laws alone, and assume nothing about how
the two clocks are coupled. That is what makes them \emph{universal}.}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{On the campaign's marginals} Within a cell, $\hat F_A(s)$ is the share of the
$100$ seeds with $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le s$ and $\hat F_D(s)$ the share with
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}} \le s$; the two bounds are the formulas above evaluated on these.
$\hat F_D$ is sub-stochastic --- a censored $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ enters it at no finite
$s$ --- so censored runs count as detector losses rather than as missing data. Unlike the censoring
interval above, which collapses because $C_H = \varnothing$, these bounds discard the joint
law on purpose: their width measures what the marginals alone leave undetermined about the
coupling, whereas $\hat P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ reads the joint law directly. At $\lambda = 8$:

(\texttt{resultats\_R2/scripts/chiffres\_QA.py}), and is not vacuous: mean width $0.195$

but only because the detector alarms in a single run out of $2\,000$ there: $\hat F_D$ stays
almost flat at $0$, and both formulas are pushed to $P_{\mathrm{miss}} \approx 1$ on their own --- exactness by degeneracy, not by
sharpness. At the weakest amplitude the estimated lower bound of $0.91$ becomes $0.88$ at
the $95\%$ level after a bootstrap resampling the $100$ seeds. There, the blind spot is
forced by the marginals alone, with no assumption on the coupling: it is not an artefact
of an independence hypothesis.

\section{Dependence Between Trees}
\label{sec:B}
% =====================================================================

To obtain the law of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \min_i \tau_i$, the manuscript treats the $M$
trees as independent, although they learn from the same stream. It states that the
resulting formula overestimates $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$, because the trees are positively
correlated, but gives no proof. This section places the independence at the level where it
holds, proves the direction of the bias, and states what it changes for the critical
ensemble size.

\subsection{Conditional Factorization}
\label{sec:factorization}

Once $S$ is fixed --- the entire trajectory $(x_t,y_t)$ known --- what still distinguishes
one tree from another is $U_i$: the Poisson weights drawn online for bagging, and the
random feature subspaces tested at each node. The first is the mechanism the manuscript
itself invokes, ``Poisson-weighted bootstrap (Oza bagging with $\lambda = 6$)''
(Section~III-G, citing~\cite{gomes2017}); the second is a defining property of the
Adaptive Random Forest in the same reference\footnote{On the R2 stream it contributes
little: with two features only, a random subspace selects roughly one of two at each node.
Diversity there rests almost entirely on the Poisson weights, which suffices --- only the
i.i.d.\ character of the $U_i$ is needed.}.

These draws are independent across trees by construction: tree $i$ never sees tree $j$'s
weights, and a tree created at replacement inherits neither weights nor structure from the
one it replaces. Since $\tau_i$ is a deterministic function of $(S, U_i)$, fixing $S$
leaves only the i.i.d.\ variation of $U_i$. Conditionally on $S$, the $\tau_i$ are
therefore independent and identically distributed, and
\[
  P(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}>s \mid S) = \prod_{i=1}^{M} P(\tau_i>s\mid S) = G_S(s)^M .
\]
The independence assumption is thus not wrong in kind --- it is stated at the wrong level.
It holds given the stream; it fails once the stream is averaged out. In the campaign, a
single seed drives both the stream and the trees' internal draws, so fixing a seed fixes
the $U_i$ as well: $G_S$ cannot be estimated across seeds, and the factorization is a
property of the model rather than a quantity measured here.

\subsection{The Direction of the Bias}
\label{sec:convexity}

Unconditioning by the law of total probability gives
$P(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}>s) = \mathbb{E}_S[G_S(s)^M]$. Since $x \mapsto x^M$ is convex on
$[0,1]$, Jensen's inequality yields
\[
  \mathbb{E}_S\big[G_S(s)^M\big] \;\ge\; \big(\mathbb{E}_S[G_S(s)]\big)^M
  = \big(1-F(s)\big)^M ,
\]
the last equality because $\mathbb{E}_S[G_S(s)] = P(\tau_1>s) = 1-F(s)$.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{Result.}
\[
  P(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}\le s) \;\le\; 1-\big(1-F(s)\big)^M
\]
The probability of having already adapted by time $s$ is at most what the independence
formula predicts: the manuscript \textbf{overestimates the repair speed}.}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Equality condition} Jensen is an equality exactly when $G_S(s)$ is almost
surely constant --- when the difficulty of adapting does not depend on which stream was
drawn. There is no reason for it here: each seed produces a different noise trajectory after the
drift. Independence is therefore not expected to be exact: it is an approximation, and a
systematically optimistic one.

\subsection{Operational Consequence}

Two quantities are affected in opposite directions, and the words below refer to different
subjects --- which is what makes the pair look contradictory. The independence formula
predicts a \emph{faster} repair than the real one, and therefore a \emph{larger}
blind-spot risk: a slower repair leaves the monitor more time, so the forest wins the race
less often than claimed. Read alone, our correction makes the blind spot \emph{less} severe
than the manuscript reports.

\paragraph{Consequence for $M_{\mathrm{crit}}$} Corollary~10 of the manuscript asks how
many trees the forest may carry before the monitor stops being reliable. Requiring
$P_{\mathrm{miss}} = 1-[1-F(\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}})]^M \le 1-\beta$ gives $[1-F]^M \ge
\beta$, hence $M\ln(1-F) \ge \ln\beta$; since $\ln(1-F)<0$ the division flips the
inequality:
\[
  M \;\le\; M_{\mathrm{crit}} := \left\lfloor \frac{\ln\beta}{\ln[1-F(\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}})]} \right\rfloor .
\]
$M_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is a \emph{ceiling} on the number of trees, not a floor --- the
physically sensible reading, since more trees mean a faster repair and a worse position
for the detector. As the true CDF lies below the independence one, the true
$P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ is smaller at every $M$: whatever ensemble size the certificate
admits, the real forest satisfies the guarantee too. The corollary is not invalidated but
\textbf{reclassified as a conservative ceiling}.

\paragraph{No coupling reverses the sign} An anti-monotone coupling between two trees would
make the repair faster than under independence. It cannot occur in an ARF: by the
conditional factorization, $\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}\{\tau_1>1\}, \mathbf{1}\{\tau_2>1\}) =
\mathrm{Var}(G_S(1)) \ge 0$, since the only channel of dependence between two trees is the
stream they share, which can only make them fail together~\cite{esary1967}. The toy
coupling, the derivation, and two reservations on how the correction reads as a statement
about the real alarm are in \hyperref[app:proofs]{Appendix~\ref*{app:proofs}}.

\section{The Alarm as a Function of the Error Path}
\label{sec:certificate}
% =====================================================================

A repair time and a detection threshold are not measured in the same units. Taking that
seriously turns the comparison of two times into a comparison between evidence and threshold: first for
detection within the horizon, through a certificate, then for the race itself.

$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ counts observations; $\lambda$ counts excess errors, since the
detector
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \max\big(0,\ S_{t-1} + e_t - \hat p_0 - \delta_P\big), \qquad S_{\tau^*} = 0,
  \label{eq:cusum}
\end{equation}
rises by nearly one unit per error and barely falls on a success. The manuscript bridges
the two by a rate: Proposition~9 sets $\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}} := \lambda/(\Delta e - \delta_P)$
and races $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against that constant instead of the observed
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$. The rate is a fiction: it decays as the forest repairs, the reset to
zero discards evidence so that the alarm date depends on the order of the errors and not
only on their total, and the threshold may never be crossed. The right object is the peak
statistic $S_{\max}(H) = \max_{t \le H} S_t$. Write
$X_t = e_t - \hat p_0 - \delta_P$ and $A(k,j) = \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} (e_t - \hat p_0)$.

\begin{proposition}[Lindley's closed form]\label{prop:lindley}
For every $j \le H$, $S_j = \max_{0 \le k \le j} \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t$, hence
\[
  S_{\max}(H) = \max_{0 \le k \le j \le H} \big[A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P\big].
\]
\end{proposition}

On the $2\,000$ campaign trajectories the closed form matches the recurrence to
$1.26 \times 10^{-11}$; the analysis script stops if this check fails.

\begin{corollary}[Certificate]\label{cor:certificate}
$S_{\max}(H) \ge \lambda$ if and only if some sub-window $]k,j] \subseteq\, ]0,H]$
satisfies $A(k,j) \ge \lambda + (j-k)\,\delta_P$. This holds on every run, with no averaging
and no distributional assumption.
\end{corollary}

\noindent The full window alone, $A(0,H) \ge \lambda + H\delta_P$, is one term of the
maximum, and therefore only sufficient.

\subsection{The Race Is Decided Before the Repair}
\label{sec:race-identity}

Let $S_{\mathrm{pre}} := \max_{0 \le t < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}} S_t$ be the highest level the
detector reaches strictly before the first replacement, with $S_{\mathrm{pre}} = 0$ when
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Race identity]\label{prop:race}
On every run and for every threshold $\lambda > 0$,
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \iff S_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge \lambda .
\]
Hence $\{S_{\mathrm{pre}} < \lambda\}$ is $\mathrm{Miss}$ together with the ties
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{det}} = \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}\}$, and by \hyperref[prop:lindley]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:lindley}}
\[
  S_{\mathrm{pre}} = \max_{0 \le k \le j < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}}
  \big[A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P\big].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}} = \inf\{t : S_t \ge \lambda\}$. If $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} <
\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, the instant $t = \tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ enters the maximum defining
$S_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and $S_t \ge \lambda$ there. Conversely, if $S_t \ge \lambda$ for some
$t < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, the first such instant is $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, which is
therefore smaller than $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$. The complement of
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}\}$ is $\mathrm{Miss}$ plus the ties, by the
convention of \hyperref[sec:miss]{Section~\ref*{sec:miss}}. The last expression is
\hyperref[prop:lindley]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:lindley}} applied up to $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} - 1$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Ties are rare: $0$, $6$ and $0$ runs at $\lambda = 8$, $25$ and $50$
(\hyperref[sec:miss]{Section~\ref*{sec:miss}}).

\paragraph{What this replaces} The manuscript races $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against
$\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}}$, a mean accumulation time. \hyperref[prop:race]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:race}} needs no
rate, no averaging and no assumption on the trees: on each run, the race compares the
excess error the forest lets through before its first replacement with the threshold.
\hyperref[sec:spre]{Section~\ref*{sec:spre}} measures that evidence, and it sets the threshold at which the race
changes hands.

\section{Campaign and Measurement}
\label{sec:campaign}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{The campaign} The R2 bench of the manuscript is rerun with its stream, its
forest ($M = 10$, internal ADWIN evaluated at every step) and its detector
($\delta_P = 0.01$): an abrupt shift of the decision boundary at $\tau^*$, on $20$
amplitudes from $\Delta e = 0.028$ to $0.498$ with $100$ seeds each, that is $2\,000$ runs
observed over $H = 2\,000$ steps after the drift, plus a drift-free arm of $2\,000$ runs.
The simulation records only the binary error $e_t$ and the tree replacement events, whose
series \hyperref[fig:joint]{Figure~\ref*{fig:joint}} traces on a common time axis. No
threshold enters the loop, so $S_t$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ and every indicator below are
recomputed offline for any $\lambda$ (\hyperref[sec:D1]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D1}}). The baseline $\hat p_0$ is
estimated over the last $3\,000$ pre-drift steps; over $1\,000$ steps, as in R2, no verdict
of this report changes.

\paragraph{Indicators} Two families are measured on every run. \emph{Repair dates}, in
steps: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$. \emph{Evidence}, in excess
errors: $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$ over the whole horizon; the short-window area $A(w)$, cut at
$w = \mathrm{round}(3 \times 18.5/\Delta e)$, three typical transients; $S_{\mathrm{pre}}$
of \hyperref[prop:race]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:race}}; and $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}) =
S_{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}}/S_{\max}(H)$, the share of the evidence already accumulated at the
first replacement. The return time $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ exists only on the seed-averaged
error and is compared amplitude by amplitude. As an external reference, the analytic
probes of the group's benchmark give the accuracy of the forest on the region whose label
changed. The area under that accuracy over the first $500$ steps measures the real
relearning, and the first instant $t_{50}$ at which it reaches one half dates it. They require the drift to be known and are
never computable online.

\paragraph{Association} A correlation pooled over the $2\,000$ runs measures the common
dependence of both quantities on $\Delta e$. Every coefficient is therefore computed within
an amplitude, on its $100$ seeds, then summarised over the $18$ amplitudes of the decision
domain $\Delta e \ge 0.10$, which excludes the replacements driven by noise at the bottom
of the grid. The coefficient is Kendall's $\tau_b$~\cite{kendall1945}, which reads on pairs
and corrects for ties; censored durations are ranked last, which is licit because the
horizon is common to all runs. Pearson's coefficient is undefined on censored values, and
Spearman's handles their tie blocks only implicitly (\hyperref[sec:D2]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D2}}). Confidence
intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1979} over the seeds, $2\,000$
resamples, and an association is called discernible when its interval excludes zero. That
rule was fixed during the analysis rather than before it; \hyperref[sec:D3]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D3}} states this
and lists the three cells on which it differs from the fixed threshold
$|\tau_b| > 0.13295637$. Shares of runs carry Wilson $95\%$ intervals~\cite{wilson1927}.

\section{What the Repair Date Measures}
\label{sec:repair-date}
% =====================================================================

The manuscript dates the adaptation of the forest at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$. Does this instant
measure what it claims and, if not, what should replace it? Three results show that it dates the repair too early and too
noisily; the last two measure what it still carries.

\subsection{The First Replacement Is a Lower Bound}
\label{sec:hierarchy}

Let $N_t$ be the number of distinct trees replaced since $\tau^*$, and
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q) = \inf\{t > \tau^* : N_t/M \ge q\} - \tau^* .
\]
$N_t$ is an integer and never decreases, so $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}$ is non-decreasing in
$q$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(1/M)$. Hence, for every $q \ge 1/M$,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \;\le\; \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q) \qquad \text{on every run.}
  \label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{equation}
With $M = 10$ the quotas $25\%$ and $75\%$ are first met at $3$ and $8$ trees; the labels
are kept and read as ``at least $3$ (resp.\ $8$) trees''.

\paragraph{A check, not a finding} Being deterministic, \eqref{eq:hierarchy} cannot fail
on the forest, only on the instrumentation. The campaign returns
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(10\%)$ on all $2\,000$ runs and no violation
among the $5\,795$ uncensored comparisons for $q \in \{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$: the offline
reconstruction is sound. What remains to be measured is the size of the gap.

\paragraph{The size of the gap} \hyperref[fig:gaps]{Figure~\ref*{fig:gaps}} gives the median gap by amplitude.
Declaring a gap above $100$ steps ($5\%$ of $H$) disqualifying --- a convention fixed before
the results were read, whose verdicts do not move anywhere in $[50, 167]$ ---
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is an acceptable substitute for $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(25\%)$ from
$\Delta e \approx 0.24$, for $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(50\%)$ from $\Delta e \approx 0.33$, and
never for $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$. Over the $18$ amplitudes where fewer than half of
the runs are censored, three quarters of the forest is renewed $167$ to $1\,368$ steps
later, that is $6.0$ to $14.3$ times $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself. The bias holds run by
run and always on the same side, so averaging over seeds does not reduce it.

\paragraph{At the bottom of the grid, replacements are noise} With no drift at all, the
forest still renews a median of $9$ trees out of $10$ within the horizon, on $14$ swap
events per run; at $\Delta e = 0.028$ the campaign records $12.5$. There the quota fills
by accident, and the two lowest amplitudes carry a different object from the rest of the
grid.

\paragraph{Error recovery comes after the first replacement} Let $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ be
the return of the seed-averaged error below $\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$, $\rho = 0.25$, held
for $20$ consecutive steps. It exists on the mean curve only, so it is compared with the
median $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, amplitude by amplitude. One could conjecture the reverse
order at low amplitude, the surviving trees absorbing a faint shock with no replacement.
Without the persistence requirement, four amplitudes violate
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le \tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$. At the two lowest the test has no power
($\rho\,\Delta e < 2\sigma$) and the crossing occurs at the very first step. At
$\Delta e = 0.141$ and $0.194$ it has power, but the crossing, at step $101$, is not
sustained, and it moves to steps $626$ and $322$ once persistence is required. No violation
remains on the $18$ amplitudes where the test has power. Where it is blind the conjecture
is not refuted either: resolving it would take about $1\,900$ seeds at $\Delta e = 0.028$.

\paragraph{Not the bias of \hyperref[sec:convexity]{Section~\ref*{sec:convexity}}} Both results find
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ optimistic, and share nothing else. \eqref{eq:hierarchy} is
combinatorial and would hold unchanged with independent trees;
\hyperref[sec:convexity]{Section~\ref*{sec:convexity}} compares two laws of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and says nothing
of $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$. Grant that correction in full, and $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ still
dates the recovery too early: the two errors compound.

\paragraph{A weak drift delays the first replacement} Just above the bottom of the grid the
order reverses: the median first replacement comes after $346$ steps at $\Delta e = 0.085$
and $263$ at $0.141$, about three times later than with no drift at all. ADWIN cuts its
window when two sub-window means differ by more than a bound that grows with the variance
of the window, so a higher error level should make the cuts driven by noise rarer. A second
drift-free control tests this by raising the error level with no change point
(\hyperref[tab:bruit]{Table~\ref*{tab:bruit}}): the first replacement recedes steadily, from $98$ steps at
$\eta = 0$ to $1\,220$ at $\eta = 0.12$. Before its first replacement, the forest runs at a
mean error of $0.054$ at $\Delta e = 0.085$; with no drift and a comparable level,
$\eta = 0.04$, it replaces its first tree after $268$ steps $[222;304]$, an interval that
overlaps the $[281;455]$ of the drift. At $\Delta e = 0.141$ the error before repair,
$0.102$, matches $\eta = 0.08$, where the first replacement waits $622$ steps against $263$
with the drift. The non-monotonicity of $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ in $\Delta e$
(\hyperref[sec:d2:features]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d2:features}}) thus has two regimes: on weak drifts the date is set by the
error level, which delays the cuts driven by noise; from $\Delta e = 0.141$ the change itself
triggers the cut. Label noise also changes what the trees learn, so the control supports
this mechanism without isolating it.

\subsection{Most of the Evidence Is Already There}
\label{sec:gshare}

At the first replacement the detector has already accumulated between $49\%$ and $76\%$ of
the evidence it will ever reach: $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$ ranges from $0.4889$ to $0.7594$
over the $18$ amplitudes of the decision domain (\hyperref[fig:rg]{Figure~\ref*{fig:rg}}, \hyperref[sec:D1]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D1}}). The first replacement
is therefore not the end of the transient but a point inside it. The fraction of
adaptation acquired at the same instant, measured on the seed-averaged error, supports no
comparable statement: it ranges from negative values to $0.885$, is not monotone in
$\Delta e$, and rests on a post-drift plateau that never reaches its theoretical level, so
it is reported in \hyperref[sec:D1]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D1}} only.

one should expect, and it is why no pooled coefficient is quoted as a result in this work.

\subsection{The Answer: Weak Links, and a Noisy Area}
\label{sec:d3:answer}

The two quantities in \hyperref[tab:strat]{Table~\ref*{tab:strat}} (\hyperref[sec:D3]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D3}}) play different roles, and conflating them
changes the answer.

$A(H)$ is the accumulated excess error over the horizon. Its association with
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is indistinguishable from zero under this protocol at all
18 amplitudes: median $+0.0526$, every confidence interval straddling zero.

$S_{\max}(H)$ is the peak of the detector path~\eqref{eq:cusum}, in the closed form of
\hyperref[prop:lindley]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:lindley}}. Its association is discernible at 15 of the 18 amplitudes, with a median of $+0.2129$, and the
confidence interval lies entirely above zero at every amplitude from $\Delta e = 0.287$
upwards. That count is the one the interval rule of \hyperref[sec:D4rule]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D4rule}} gives; the
\texttt{discernable} column implements the threshold rule and would give $14$, the
difference being the single cell $\Delta e = 0.287$, whose interval $[+0.0027 ; +0.2519]$
excludes zero while its $\tau_b$ of $0.1308$ falls just under the threshold.

The distinction matters because it is $S_{\max}(H)$ that decides the alarm, not $A(H)$.
The alarm is by definition the crossing of $\lambda$ by $S_{\max}$. A claim that
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ carries no information about the evidence offered to the detector is
attached to the wrong object if it rests on $A(H)$ alone.

\paragraph{Why $A(H)$ shows nothing} $A(H)$ sums $e_t - \hat p_0$ over $2\,000$ steps, so an
error on the estimated baseline is multiplied by $2\,000$. Within a stratum,
$\tau_b(\hat p_0, A(H))$ has a median of $-0.4741$ over the 18 amplitudes: the runs whose
baseline was estimated high show the least excess error, whatever the forest did. The
baseline accounts for a median share of $0.51$ of the run-to-run variance of $A(H)$, and up
to $0.88$. Over the short window $w = \mathrm{round}(3 \times 18.5/\Delta e)$ of
\hyperref[sec:campaign]{Section~\ref*{sec:campaign}}, the same coefficient falls to $-0.0639$, and an association
with $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ appears: median $+0.2313$, with a bootstrap interval excluding
zero at 13 of the 18 amplitudes. This is exploratory --- the window was added after the
table above was read --- and it is reproduced by \texttt{chiffres\_QD.py --sous D3w}. The
null on $A(H)$ therefore says more about $A(H)$ than about $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$.

\noindent The answer, stated sharply:

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ shows \textbf{no discernible
association} with the full-horizon excess error $A(H)$ at any of the 18 amplitudes, but
about half of the variance of $A(H)$ is baseline noise multiplied by $H$. Over the
transient window alone, $A(w)$, the association is \textbf{weak but real}: median
$\tau_b = +0.2313$, discernible at 13 of 18 amplitudes (exploratory). It is of the same
order with $S_{\max}(H)$, the quantity that determines whether the alarm fires: median
$\tau_b = +0.2129$, discernible at 15 of 18 amplitudes, every amplitude from
$\Delta e = 0.287$ upwards without exception, and at none below. Ties on
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ are too rare to move any of these verdicts
(\hyperref[sec:D2]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D2}}).}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Censoring drives none of this} Recomputing every coefficient on complete cases
instead of ranking censored runs last changes no coefficient by more than $0.0403$, on
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$, the one comparator whose censoring exceeds half of a cell
inside the domain (\hyperref[sec:d3:censure]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d3:censure}}).

\subsection{The Race, Measured}

\subsection{The Evidence Before the Repair Sets the Transition}
\label{sec:spre}


\hyperref[prop:race]{Proposition~\ref*{prop:race}} holds run by run on the campaign: at $\lambda = 8$, $25$ and
$50$, the runs in which $S_{\mathrm{pre}} \ge \lambda$ are exactly those in which the
detector arrives first, in agreement on $2\,000$ runs out of $2\,000$
(\texttt{matrice\_correlations.py}). \hyperref[tab:spre]{Table~\ref*{tab:spre}} reads the race through that
quantity.

\paragraph{The transition threshold} Above $\Delta e = 0.10$ the median $S_{\mathrm{pre}}$
lies between $13.1$ and $19.8$. A threshold below that level is reached before the repair
in most runs; a threshold above it rarely is. This is the transition the manuscript observes
at $\lambda^* \approx 15$ to $25$ and leaves to future work: at $\lambda = 15$ the detector
still arrives first in $36.3\%$ of runs, at $\lambda = 20$ in $8.1\%$.

\paragraph{The paradoxical signature} From $\Delta e = 0.141$ upwards the median
$S_{\mathrm{pre}}$ decreases as the drift grows, from $19.8$ to $13.1$, because the first
replacement comes sooner. At a threshold between these values the detector therefore loses
more often on stronger drifts: at $\lambda = 15$ its share falls from $81\%$ to $18\%$ over
the same range. The paradox is a decreasing evidence before the repair, read against a
fixed threshold.

\paragraph{At the bottom of the grid} At $\Delta e = 0.028$ the median $S_{\mathrm{pre}}$
is $1.7$: the excess error is too small for the detector to accumulate anything before the
forest replaces a tree, and that replacement is itself driven by noise
(\hyperref[sec:e:nodrift]{Section~\ref*{sec:e:nodrift}}). This is the weak-signal regime of the manuscript rather than
its blind spot.

\paragraph{The full window proves little} Applied to the whole horizon,
\hyperref[cor:certificate]{Corollary~\ref*{cor:certificate}} pays a toll $H\delta_P = 20$, which turns the thresholds into
raw areas of $28$, $45$ and $70$. What a run has to offer against these is the
evidence budget of \hyperref[sec:budget]{Section~\ref*{sec:budget}}: about $18$ units whatever the amplitude on the
manuscript's rectangle and fit (Sections~III-C and III-D there), and a measured median
between $17.3$ and $30.8$ over the transient window. These are what a typical run supplies,
not a ceiling on any single run. Over the whole horizon the typical run falls short of all
three requirements, so only runs well above the average can meet them, and the higher the
bar the fewer they are. $A(0,H)$ reaches $28$ in $473$ of the $2\,000$
runs, $45$ in $66$, and $70$ in none. The full window thus certifies detection for
$23.7\%$ of runs at $\lambda = 8$ (Wilson $95\%$ interval $[21.8;25.6]$), $3.3\%$ at
$\lambda = 25$ ($[2.6;4.2]$) and none at $\lambda = 50$. Failing a sufficient condition
proves nothing: the full window is simply the wrong place to look.

\paragraph{The short window} Anchoring instead on $w = \mathrm{round}(3 \times 18.5/\Delta e)$
steps after the drift and checking $S_{\max}(w) \ge \lambda$ gives
\hyperref[tab:certificate]{Table~\ref*{tab:certificate}}. The window being fixed in advance and shorter than $H$, the
certificate is sufficient for detection within $H$ but not necessary --- an alarm may fire
after $w$ --- so it can never exceed observed detection: what informs is their gap. At $\lambda = 8$ it vanishes (mean $0.0005$ over the $20$ amplitudes): the alarm
fires inside the transient. At $\lambda = 25$ it reaches $0.26$ at $\Delta e = 0.141$:
those alarms fire after the transient and cannot be credited to the drift window. At
$\lambda = 50$ the certificate is $0$ at every amplitude.

\subsection{An Almost Constant Evidence Budget}
\label{sec:budget}

The manuscript's stylized transient is a rectangle: an excess error $\Delta e$ over a width
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$. Combined with its fit
$\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}] \approx 18.5\,\Delta e^{-0.98}$, this predicts
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[A] \;\approx\; \Delta e \cdot \mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}]
  \;\approx\; 18.5\,\Delta e^{\,0.02},
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
which varies by $5.9\%$, from $17.2$ to $18.2$, while the amplitude varies by a factor of
$17.7$: the forest repairs all the faster as the shock is violent, and a violent shock
leaves the detector no more evidence than a slow drift. The tolerance makes this worse
where it matters. $\delta_P$ removes a share $\delta_P/\Delta e$ of the budget, $35.5\%$ at
$\Delta e = 0.028$ against $2.0\%$ at $0.498$, leaving a usable budget of $11.1$ to $17.9$.
None of the full-window requirements of $28$, $45$ or $70$ is within reach on average, and
observing longer widens the deficit, since the toll grows linearly in $H$ while the budget
does not.


\paragraph{The measurement confirms the fact, not the derivation} Over
$\Delta e \in [0.085;0.498]$, where the amplitude varies by a factor of $5.8$, the measured
short-window area $A(w)$ varies by $1.78$ (\hyperref[fig:budget]{Figure~\ref*{fig:budget}}): the compensation is
real, though weaker than the $1.04$ predicted, and the model underestimates the area
mid-grid, $30.8$ measured against $18.0$ at $\Delta e = 0.243$. Including
$\Delta e = 0.028$, where the short window nearly covers the horizon and the signal sits
below the noise floor, the factor becomes $11.4$, so the restriction is part of the result.
Both hypotheses stacked in~\eqref{eq:budget} fail. The fit holds only mid-grid: measured
over predicted $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $0.19$ at $\Delta e = 0.028$, $2.08$ at $0.141$,
$1.02$ at $0.287$ and $0.76$ at the top. The rectangle, recomputed from the
\emph{measured} $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, overestimates $A(w)$ by $23\%$ to $43\%$ below
$\Delta e = 0.19$ and underestimates it by $6.9\%$ to $41.1\%$ above, changing sign in
between: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is not the width of the transient, as
\hyperref[sec:hierarchy]{Section~\ref*{sec:hierarchy}} predicts. At large amplitude a third failure appears. The
post-drift task is easier than the original one, the error settles below its baseline ---
on $1\,934$ of the $2\,000$ steps at $\Delta e = 0.498$ --- and $A(H)$ turns negative from
$\Delta e = 0.482$, down to $-20.8$.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{Result.} The evidence the repair leaves to the
detector is nearly independent of the drift's amplitude --- a factor of $1.8$ against $5.8$
--- and the tolerance erodes it most at low amplitude. This rests on direct measurement:
the manuscript's derivation, its exponent $0.02$ and its rectangle, is scaffolding that the
measurement replaces rather than supports.}}
\end{center}

\subsection{Detecting Is Not Winning}

\paragraph{Control} $S_t$ does not depend on $\lambda$, so a single offline pass over
each trajectory yields the first crossing for every threshold. The sweep must reproduce
the campaign exactly at its three original thresholds, and it does: the alarm counts
of \hyperref[tab:aveugle]{Table~\ref*{tab:aveugle}} are recovered to the run. Nothing below is readable without
this check.


The campaign of $2\,000$ runs is read at six thresholds (\hyperref[tab:seuils]{Table~\ref*{tab:seuils}}). The drift-free arm of \hyperref[sec:e:nodrift]{Section~\ref*{sec:e:nodrift}} gives what the same threshold costs when
nothing happens.


\paragraph{The two columns come apart} At $\lambda = 15$ the alarm still fires in
$92.9\%$ of runs, barely below the $95.3\%$ of $\lambda = 8$, but it arrives first in only
$36.3\%$ of them. Almost two thirds of its alarms are raised after the forest has already
replaced a tree --- the \emph{Zombie alarms} of the manuscript. Reading the detection
column alone would suggest that a moderate threshold escapes the blind spot. It does not.

\paragraph{The paradoxical signature survives at $\lambda = 15$} Amplitude by amplitude
(\hyperref[tab:spre]{Table~\ref*{tab:spre}}), the share of runs won by the detector climbs with the amplitude at
$\lambda = 8$, from $62\%$ to $99\%$; at $\lambda = 15$ it peaks at $81\%$ then falls to
$18\%$, and at $\lambda = 20$ it collapses to $1\%$. That is the signature the manuscript
reports at $\lambda = 25$, and the transition $\lambda^* \approx 15$ to $25$ it announces is
therefore confirmed on the race, not contradicted.

\paragraph{What the false-alarm column adds} The manuscript grants that a hyper-reactive
threshold reaches a safe zone but states that it ``sacrifices false-alarm robustness''. On
this bench that cost is measured rather than assumed: $\lambda = 8$ raises an alarm in
$83$ of $2\,000$ runs where nothing changes, $4.15\%$. This is a share of runs with at least one alarm over a finite horizon, not a
per-test error rate, and it has no conventional level to be compared with. So on the
Bernoulli bench the lowest threshold wins nine races out of ten for that share of false alarms, and $\lambda = 12$ still wins $78.8\%$ of them for $0.55\%$: the admissible
set is not empty here.

\paragraph{Three reservations} The false-alarm rate is measured over $2\,000$ steps on
$2\,000$ runs; over a longer deployment such alarms accumulate, and the upper end of the
Wilson interval at $\lambda = 8$ is $5.12\%$. The bench is homoscedastic, so this says
nothing about the \emph{Fundamental Tension} the manuscript establishes on
ARMA--GARCH streams, where bounding false alarms under clustered volatility is what empties
the admissible set. And at the weakest amplitude no threshold wins a single run, which
\hyperref[sec:A]{Section~\ref*{sec:A}} establishes without any dependence assumption.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================


\hyperref[tab:contributions]{Table~\ref*{tab:contributions}} summarises the argument against the manuscript. The
manuscript's phenomenon survives every correction; what changes is its mechanism and its
reach. The blind spot is a comparison, run by run, between the evidence the forest lets
through before its first replacement and a fixed threshold. The repair date that the
manuscript uses is the earliest possible one, and it is only weakly tied to the evidence
that decides the race or to the damage.

\subsection{What Was Not Done}

\begin{itemize}
  \item One campaign, at $M = 10$, on a homoscedastic Bernoulli bench: no result as a
    function of the ensemble size, and nothing on the heteroscedastic streams where the
    manuscript locates its \emph{Fundamental Tension}.
  \item The decision rule on $\tau_b$ was fixed during the analysis. The short-window area,
    $S_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and the fourteen-indicator matrix were added after the stratified
    analysis and are exploratory; no correction for multiplicity is applied.
  \item $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ exists on seed-averaged curves only and is not correlated run by
    run.
  \item $\mathrm{Var}(G_S)$ is not estimated: a single seed drives the stream and the trees,
    so the bias of \hyperref[sec:B]{Section~\ref*{sec:B}} has a sign but no size.
  \item The supervisor's additional questions --- the shape of the error after the drift,
    the window of the evidence area, the variance of the error across trees, a z-score of
    the error drop --- are treated in a separate working document, on the same campaign and
    on a per-tree pilot of $100$ runs. Its conclusions agree with this report and are not
    merged into it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{An Open Question: a Reference for Adaptation}

Every indicator on the detector's side is a function of the ensemble error, and the error
mixes two things: how much the forest has relearned, and how hard the new task is. At the
top of the grid the new task is easier and the error settles below its pre-drift baseline.
On the analytic probes, the forest there relearns the changed region early, yet in most
runs it permanently loses the rare region above the new boundary, a loss too small to
appear in the error (journal of the group's benchmark, third control of the analytic
reference). The short-window area is the best
measure of damage among ours, but a reference for adaptation itself needs the probes,
which are not available online.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Two corrections requested on the manuscript turned out to be one. Racing the first tree
replacement against a nominal detection time compares a count of observations with a
count of errors, and dating the repair at the first replacement picks the earliest and
noisiest date available. Written in the detector's own unit, the race becomes exact and
needs no assumption on the trees: the detector arrives first if and only if the evidence
accumulated before the first replacement reaches the threshold. That single quantity
accounts for the threshold at which the race changes hands and for the paradoxical
signature, while the first replacement tracks neither the evidence nor the damage beyond
a weak association. The blind spot survives this analysis. What it loses is its reading
as a race between two clocks; what remains open is a reference against which adaptation
itself could be measured.

\subsection{Fréchet Bounds}

\paragraph{Lower bound} For a fixed $s \in \mathbb{N}$, the event
$\{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}\le s < \tau_{\mathrm{det}}\}$ implies
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}<\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, so it is a \emph{subset} of $\mathrm{Miss}$.
Write $A = \{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le s\}$ and $B = \{\tau_{\mathrm{det}} > s\}$, so
$P(A) = F_A(s)$ and $P(B) = 1 - F_D(s)$. Fréchet's inequality follows from
$P(A \cup B) \le 1$:
\[
  P(A \cap B) \;=\; P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cup B) \;\ge\; P(A) + P(B) - 1 ,
\]
and since a probability is non-negative,
$P(A \cap B) \ge \max\big(0, P(A)+P(B)-1\big)$. Substituting,
$P(A) + P(B) - 1 = F_A(s) - F_D(s)$. The inclusion holding for every $s$, we keep the
largest value:
\[
  P_{\mathrm{miss}} \;\ge\; \max\Big(0,\ \sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}} \big[F_A(s)-F_D(s)\big]\Big).
\]

\paragraph{Upper bound} Here we proceed by \emph{superset}. We claim
\[
  \mathrm{Miss} \;\subseteq\; \{\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}\le s\} \cup \{\tau_{\mathrm{det}}> s+1\},
\]
and prove it by taking a run in $\mathrm{Miss}$ and splitting on where
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ falls. If $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le s$ the run lies in the first set
and nothing more is needed. If $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \ge s+1$, then
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}} > \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \ge s+1$ puts it in the second. In words:
either the forest was early, or the alarm --- which comes after it --- was late. Boole's
subadditivity, then optimizing over $s$ and truncating at $1$, gives
\[
  P_{\mathrm{miss}} \;\le\; \min\Big(1,\ \inf_{s\in\mathbb{N}} \big[F_A(s) + 1 - F_D(s+1)\big]\Big).
\]

\paragraph{Verification on a toy case} Take both clocks uniform on $\{1,2\}$, so
$F_A = F_D$ with $F_A(1) = \tfrac12$. Then $\mathrm{Miss}$ requires
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = 1$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} = 2$, and its probability depends
entirely on the coupling: $0$ when the two clocks always agree, $0.25$ under
independence, $0.5$ when one is $1$ exactly as the other is $2$. Identical marginals,
three answers. The formulas give a lower bound of $0$ and an upper bound of $0.5$
(attained at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$), both reached: the interval is exactly the set of
achievable values, and nothing sharper can be had from marginals alone. Assuming
independence, as the manuscript does, is therefore not a harmless convenience --- it
selects one point out of $[0,\,0.5]$.

\subsection{Lindley's Closed Form}

The closed form is Lindley's recursion for the waiting time in a single-server
queue~\cite{lindley1952}; a direct proof is given for completeness, because it also names a window that attains the
maximum.

\begin{proof}
Fix $j \le H$. \emph{Every window ending at $j$ is dominated.} The recurrence~\eqref{eq:cusum}
gives $S_t \ge S_{t-1} + X_t$ at every step, so for $0 \le k \le j$, telescoping from $k+1$ to $j$
and using $S_k \ge 0$,
\[
  \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t \;\le\; S_j - S_k \;\le\; S_j .
\]
\emph{One window attains $S_j$.} Let $k^\star = \max\{t \le j : S_t = 0\}$, well defined since
$S_0 = 0$. For $k^\star < t \le j$ we have $S_t > 0$, so the floor at zero
in~\eqref{eq:cusum} is not active and $S_t = S_{t-1} + X_t$. Telescoping with
$S_{k^\star} = 0$ gives $S_j = \sum_{t=k^\star+1}^{j} X_t$, the empty sum when $k^\star = j$.
Hence $S_j = \max_{0 \le k \le j} \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t$. Since
$\sum_{t=k+1}^{j} X_t = A(k,j) - (j-k)\,\delta_P$, the maximum over $j \le H$ gives the
second form.
\end{proof}

\noindent The slack in the first inequality is exactly
\[
  \sum_{t=k+1}^{j} \max\big(0,\ -(S_{t-1} + X_t)\big),
\]
the evidence the counter discards each
time the floor at zero is strictly active inside $]k,j]$. A window containing such a step
falls strictly short of $S_j$, and extending the window back from $k^\star$ to some
$k' < k^\star$ adds $\sum_{t=k'+1}^{k^\star} X_t \le S_{k^\star} - S_{k'} = -S_{k'} \le 0$.
A best window therefore starts at the last zero of the counter before its end. The recurrence
is Kadane's maximum-subarray algorithm with the empty subarray allowed: the detector computes
online, in $O(H)$ steps, the best of the $O(H^2)$ windows of the horizon.


\subsection{Two Reservations on the Independence Correction}

The comparison holds at a \emph{fixed}
$\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}}$, which Proposition~9 makes a constant. Read as a claim about the
real blind-spot risk it assumes more, since the real $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is computed on
the ensemble's error stream and so depends on how correlated the trees are. Both effects
in fact lower $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ --- a slower repair alarms earlier, and weaker variance
reduction leaves a burstier stream that breaches $\lambda$ on noise alone --- so the sign
holds, but part of the decrease is bought with alarms that win the race without detecting
anything.

And the corollary is not rescued. At $\Delta e = 0.33$ the manuscript measures
$\widehat F(\tau^*_{\mathrm{det}}) = 0.49$: one tree alone already gives
$P_{\mathrm{miss}} = 0.49$, ten times the $5\%$ that $\beta = 0.95$ allows, whence
$M_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0$. Our correction states the true ceiling is $\ge 0$, which says
nothing --- rescuing the certificate would require $\ge 1$. The status changes, the verdict
does not.

\subsection{The Correlation Trap}

Could the dependence run the other way and reverse the conclusion? On the toy case of
two trees uniform on $\{1,2\}$, an anti-monotone coupling ($\tau_1 = 1 \iff \tau_2 = 2$)
gives $P(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} \le 1) = 1$ against $0.75$ under independence --- a
\emph{faster} adaptation. Its covariance is
$0 - \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12 = -\tfrac14$, strictly negative.

No ARF can realize it. By the law of total covariance, with
$X=\mathbf{1}\{\tau_1{>}1\}$ and $Y=\mathbf{1}\{\tau_2{>}1\}$, the conditional term
vanishes by \hyperref[sec:factorization]{Section~\ref*{sec:factorization}} and both conditional expectations equal
$G_S(1)$, so
\[
  \mathrm{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathrm{Var}\big(G_S(1)\big) \;\ge\; 0 .
\]

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\columnwidth}{\textbf{The trap defused.} The only channel of dependence
between two trees is the stream they share, and sharing a stream can only make them fail
together --- never in opposition. The covariance is therefore never negative, and the
anti-monotone coupling is structurally out of reach. This is not a missing link in
\hyperref[sec:convexity]{Section~\ref*{sec:convexity}}, whose proof never invokes the sign of the dependence: it is a
second consequence of the same conditional factorization, and it closes the one objection
a reader would raise against it.}}
\end{center}

\noindent Both delays are counted from $\tau^*$ and take integer values, a fact the
Fréchet bounds of \hyperref[sec:A]{Section~\ref*{sec:A}} rely on. Every measured figure in this report comes from the
instrumented campaign of $20$ amplitudes $\times\ 100$ seeds $= 2\,000$ runs,
$H = 2\,000$, $M = 10$, documented in \texttt{resultats\_R2/}; the exceptions are the
drift-free arm of $2\,000$ runs of \hyperref[sec:e:nodrift]{Section~\ref*{sec:e:nodrift}}, the analytic probes, recorded
on a rerun of the same campaign, and the ten fictitious runs of the counter-example in
\hyperref[sec:d2:contre]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d2:contre}}, all signalled where they are used.

\paragraph*{Intervals in brackets} A share of runs, $k$ out of $n$, is an estimate of a
probability $p$ from a finite sample, and is reported with its Wilson $95\%$ interval,
$[\ell;u]$: the set of values of $p$ under which observing $k$ successes out of $n$ would
not be rejected at the $5\%$ level. Concretely, $p$ is retained whenever $k$ lies within
$1.96$ standard deviations of $np$, the standard deviation $\sqrt{np(1-p)}$ being taken
under $p$ itself and not under the estimate $k/n$; solving that inequality in $p$ gives
the two bounds. Two consequences matter for reading the tables. The interval never
collapses at the edges --- $0$ out of $100$ gives $[0.00;0.04]$, not $[0;0]$, since a
rare event can go unobserved in $100$ trials --- and two shares whose intervals overlap
substantially cannot be told apart, whereas disjoint intervals mark a genuine difference.
With $n = 100$ the half-width is about $\pm 0.10$ near $p = 0.5$ and narrows towards
$0$ and $1$.

\section{Instrumenting the Repair, the Error and the Detector}
\label{sec:D1}
% =====================================================================

The fraction of replaced trees, the global error and the internal state of the detector
are traced here on one time axis. This section produces those three series and
the four temporal markers read off them; what they are worth as measurements of the
adaptation is settled in \hyperref[sec:D2]{Appendices~\ref*{sec:D2}} and~\ref{sec:D3}. Every figure quoted is
reproduced by \texttt{resultats\_R2/scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} and \texttt{derives\_QD.py}.

\subsection{What Is Recorded and What Is Recomputed}

The simulation writes raw material only: the error trajectory $e_t \in \{0,1\}$, the tree
replacement events, and the $3\,000$ pre-drift steps. No threshold and no $\lambda$
appears anywhere in the simulation loop.

That is deliberate. The external detector reads the error trajectory and never acts on
the forest, so $\lambda$ has no influence on the dynamics. Once the \texttt{break} is
removed, the three scenarios of R2 produce runs identical to the bit. One campaign
replaces three, and $S_t$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$, $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$ are swept
offline for every threshold and any $\delta_P$. Freezing a threshold inside the loop
would have cost one campaign per threshold, and at $\lambda = 50$ it would have produced
a table whose $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ column is censored in $99.95\%$ of runs. It is also
what makes the continuous threshold sweep of \hyperref[sec:E]{Section~\ref*{sec:E}} cost nothing.

Two conventions are used throughout and stated once here. The detector is created at
$\tau^*$ with $S_{\tau^*} = 0$, and every duration is counted from $\tau^*$. The two
amplitudes below $\Delta e = 0.10$ lie outside the decision domain fixed in
\hyperref[sec:D4rule]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D4rule}} and are shaded on every figure that sweeps $\Delta e$.

The hierarchy~\eqref{eq:hierarchy} of \hyperref[sec:hierarchy]{Section~\ref*{sec:hierarchy}} is used throughout,
and with it the identity $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = \tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(1/M)$: that is what
makes the first row of \hyperref[tab:agregation]{Table~\ref*{tab:agregation}} tautological rather than measured.

\subsection{Reading the Joint Figure}

\paragraph{No temporal smoothing} $e_t$ is binary. The only averaging applied is the
inter-seed mean, taken step by step, with no sliding filter anywhere. A moving average of
length $L$ shifts the curve by half its support and destroys the synchronisation with
$\tau^*$, which is what this figure exists to display. At the top of the grid the median
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $28$ steps, so a $50$-step filter would displace the curve by
nearly the whole quantity being measured.

\paragraph{The four markers} $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ is plotted at $\lambda = 8$, the only
threshold whose censoring stays in the minority ($4.70\%$, against $61.10\%$ at
$\lambda = 25$ and $99.95\%$ at $\lambda = 50$). At the other two thresholds a median
delay would rest on a minority of runs, so none is published. $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ carries
the persistence requirement whose necessity \hyperref[sec:hierarchy]{Section~\ref*{sec:hierarchy}} establishes: four
amplitudes cross the threshold without the crossing being sustained, and the marker is
dashed where the test has no power.

\paragraph{Replacement dynamics} At $t = 100$ the median $N_t/M$ ranges from $0.00$ to
$0.70$ across amplitudes, with an interquartile range of $0.00$ to $0.30$; at $t = 400$,
from $0.10$ to $0.90$. By the end of the horizon the median runs from $0.60$ to $1.00$,
and the proportion of runs whose forest has been entirely renewed goes from $2\%$ to
$94\%$ depending on amplitude.

\subsection{The Baseline and Its Estimation Window}
\label{sec:d1:socle}

The baseline $\hat p_0$ enters $S_t$ directly, and therefore governs $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$,
$A(H)$, $S_{\max}(H)$ and every censoring rate reported in this work. It is reported on
both estimation windows: $1\,000$ pre-drift steps, reproducing R2 as published,
and $3\,000$ steps for the analysis. Since $3\,000$ pre-drift steps are recorded per run,
this is a purely offline recomputation. As a control, the baseline recomputed at $3\,000$
steps reproduces the campaign value with a maximum deviation of exactly $0$.

\paragraph{Against the theoretical baseline} The pre-drift target

entirely approximation error, produced by axis-parallel splits approximating an oblique
boundary. The detector monitors departures from a reference level that belongs to the
classifier rather than to the problem.

\subsection{What Is Acquired at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$}

Two quantities are measured at the instant $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$: the fraction of
adaptation already acquired, $R(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$, and the fraction of evidence
already accumulated by the detector, $G(\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}})$. $G$ is computed run by run,
$R$ per amplitude on the mean curve, and $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is the inter-seed median.

\paragraph{Answer} $G$ ranges from $0.4889$ to $0.7594$ over the 18 amplitudes of the
decision domain: between half and three quarters of the evidence is already available to
the detector when the first tree is replaced. On the full grid it falls to $0.1023$, and
that restriction is published with the figure.

$R$ supports no such single sentence. It runs from $-0.1882$ to $+0.8853$ on the grid,
from $-0.1882$ to $+0.5883$ on the decision domain, and it is not monotone in $\Delta e$.
At $\Delta e = 0.085$ it reaches $0.885$: nearly all of the adaptation is acquired when
the first tree is replaced, the opposite of what one would expect. At $\Delta e = 0.141$
it is $0.588$. The reading ``early in the adaptation, late in the race'' is not supported
by the table and is not used here. What the measurement supports is narrower: at
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, between $49\%$ and $76\%$ of the evidence is accumulated, while the
fraction of adaptation acquired varies from negative to $89\%$ with no monotone dependence
on amplitude.

\paragraph{A negative fraction of adaptation} $R$ is negative at five amplitudes
($\Delta e = 0.243$, $0.436$, $0.494$, $0.496$, $0.498$), reaching $-0.1882$. The window
used to estimate the post-drift plateau is bounded by $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}/2$ precisely to
keep it from overlapping the adaptation it measures, and the bound is active at those
amplitudes (the window drops to $43$, $17$, $14$, $14$, $14$ steps respectively). The
symptom persists anyway. A negative $R$ means the mean error at $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ sits
\emph{above} the plateau estimated just after the drift: the error is still rising when
the first tree is replaced. Calling the estimation artefact repaired would be false.

\paragraph{The plateau never reaches its theoretical value} The control of the measured
plateau against $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ fails on all $20$ amplitudes, with a deviation
between $-0.0271$ and $-0.0080$, always negative. Two mechanisms account for it, and both
point at the baseline.

First, $\hat p_0$ is itself partly a product of the replacement mechanism. On the
drift-free arm of the campaign --- $2\,000$ runs with no change point at all, reported in
full in \hyperref[sec:e:nodrift]{Section~\ref*{sec:e:nodrift}} --- the forest renews most of itself over the horizon anyway,
for a mean error of $0.0228$. Replacement churn under pure noise is the normal regime. The
same forest with its detectors disabled, which never replaces anything, runs at $0.0097$ on
$200$ drift-free seeds, against $0.0226$ for the standard forest on the same seeds: under
pure noise the replacement mechanism more than doubles the error
(\texttt{exp\_QCD\_temoins\_bruit.py}). An exploratory measurement on $12$ runs in the
group's journal had given $0.0125$.

Second, at large amplitudes the post-drift task is easier than the original one, so the
error does not settle at $\hat p_0 + \Delta e$ at all --- the same mechanism that drives
$A(H)$ negative in \hyperref[sec:budget]{Section~\ref*{sec:budget}}. The stylised step model fails at the
top of the grid, and that is a measurement rather than a conjecture.

\subsection{The Race, Amplitude by Amplitude}
\label{sec:d1:course}

\hyperref[tab:race]{Table~\ref*{tab:race}} gives $P_{\mathrm{miss}}$ pooled over the whole grid. The
instrumentation makes visible what that pooling hides, and the reading below is the same
race under its other orientation: the detector wins exactly when the forest does not.

At $\lambda = 8$, $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} < \tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ in $95.44\%$ of the $1\,906$
uncensored runs, or $90.95\%$ of all $2\,000$ runs when censored ones are imputed as
losses. Among those $2\,000$ runs, $34$ have $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}} = 0$: a tree is replaced
at the change point itself, and the detector loses by construction, since
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ cannot precede the drift. They occur at every amplitude, three at
$\Delta e = 0.028$ down to one at the top of the grid, and \hyperref[fig:boxplot]{Figure~\ref*{fig:boxplot}} plots
them explicitly. At $\lambda = 25$ the proportion falls to $6.56\%$, and at $\lambda = 50$

dominated by the 18 easy amplitudes out of 20. The blind spot is therefore a property of
the pair $(\lambda, \Delta e)$ rather than of $\lambda$ alone: even at the most sensitive
threshold tested, it is fully present at the bottom of the grid.

\section{Choosing the Association Coefficient}
\label{sec:D2}
% =====================================================================

Measuring how $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ associates with the real adaptation requires a
coefficient, and which one is not a detail here: the quantities to be
correlated are durations measured on a finite horizon, and two of the three usual
coefficients are disqualified by that alone. The counter-example of
\hyperref[sec:d2:contre]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d2:contre}} comes from \texttt{scripts/contre\_exemple\_kendall.py},
which computes both coefficients twice, once from their definition and once through
\texttt{scipy}, so that each controls the other.

\subsection{Three Features of the Data}
\label{sec:d2:features}

\paragraph{Non-monotonicity} The median $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is $118$ steps at
$\Delta e = 0.028$, rises to $346$ at $\Delta e = 0.085$, then falls steadily to $28$ at
the top of the grid: a range of $28$ to $346$, a factor of $12.4$, and not monotone in
$\Delta e$. The rise is not an artefact of the median. The means move the same way
($162.7$ then $385.9$), and the bootstrap confidence intervals of the two medians are
disjoint, $[92.5 ; 158.5]$ against $[281 ; 455]$ (\hyperref[tab:ic-medianes]{Table~\ref*{tab:ic-medianes}}). The
\emph{location} of the peak is less certain: the interval at $\Delta e = 0.141$,
$[231.5 ; 307]$, overlaps the one at $\Delta e = 0.085$ over $[281 ; 307]$, so the two
cells are not separated by the rule used here and the claim is that a peak exists at low
amplitude, not that it sits at one particular cell. That negative statement is weak, and
the control quantifies how weak: at a ratio of medians of $1.32$, which is exactly
$346/263$, the rule separates two samples of $100$ in only $23.4\%$ of $500$ repetitions
on a log-normal law. Failing to separate these two cells is therefore close to
uninformative, whereas the separation of $\Delta e = 0.028$ from $\Delta e = 0.085$ is
not: the rule falsely separates two samples of the same law in $1.0\%$ of repetitions.
The relationship is therefore worse than merely curved, which would already understate
Pearson's $r$. No single linear coefficient summarises it, and its sign depends on which
part of the grid is included. \hyperref[sec:e:nodrift]{Section~\ref*{sec:e:nodrift}} traces the rise to the error level,
which delays the replacements driven by noise. \hyperref[fig:boxplot]{Figure~\ref*{fig:boxplot}} shows the full
distribution at every amplitude.

\paragraph{Extreme values} At low amplitude the durations are large and widely dispersed.
Pearson's coefficient is a ratio of moments and is not robust, so a handful of runs in the
tail move it substantially. Both rank coefficients are unaffected.

\paragraph{Administrative censoring at $H$} A duration never reached within the horizon
has no numerical value. $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ is censored in $9.10\%$ of runs
overall and in up to $78\%$ at a single amplitude; $\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ at $\lambda = 50$
is censored in $99.95\%$.

\paragraph{Which survives stratification} \hyperref[sec:D3]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D3}} holds $\Delta e$ constant
within a stratum, and the three features do not survive that equally. The first does not
survive at all: the non-monotonicity is non-monotonicity \emph{in $\Delta e$}, and within
a stratum $\Delta e$ is fixed by construction. The other two survive, and they are
objections of different kinds. Extreme values make Pearson's coefficient inefficient: it
can still be computed, it is merely unstable and moves with a few runs. Censoring makes it
undefined, because a censored run has no numerical value to enter the sum of products, so
the coefficient cannot be formed without first inventing one. The second objection carries
the argument, since the first is a matter of degree and the second a matter of definition.
It is also the objection that bites on our data, where $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ is
censored in nearly four runs out of five at $\Delta e = 0.085$.

\subsection{Why Censored Runs May Be Ranked Last}
\label{sec:d2:rang}

Both rank coefficients handle censoring the same way and for the same reason. The horizon
$H = 2\,000$ is identical for every run, so a duration not reached within it genuinely
exceeds every observed duration, and censored runs may be placed at the last rank, tied
among themselves.

The argument depends entirely on the horizon being common, and it is stated here because
every coefficient in this report relies on it. If $H$ varied from run to run, a censored
value would only be known to exceed \emph{its own} horizon: a run censored at $H = 500$
and an observed run of $1\,200$ steps could not be ordered at all, and no imputation to a
shared last rank would be licit. Under variable horizons the correct tool is a
censoring-aware estimator such as Harrell's $C$ index. The convention used here rests on a
property of this experimental design and does not transfer. \hyperref[sec:d3:censure]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d3:censure}}
checks what it changes in practice: nothing that carries a verdict.

That common horizon is also where the choice between Spearman and Kendall becomes
decisive.

\subsection{Definition of $\tau_b$, and Two Tie Structures}

For $n$ pairs, let $C$ be the number of concordant pairs, $D$ the discordant ones, and
$n_0 = n(n-1)/2$. Let $t_x$ count the pairs tied on $x$ only and $t_y$ those tied on $y$
only, pairs tied on both sides being counted in each. Then
\begin{equation}
  \tau_b = \frac{C - D}{\sqrt{(n_0 - t_x)(n_0 - t_y)}}.
  \label{eq:taub}
\end{equation}
The denominator distinguishes $\tau_b$ from the ordinary $\tau_a$: each tie block is
removed from the count on the side where it occurs, so the coefficient is normalised by
the pairs that could in principle have been ordered on both sides. Spearman's $\rho$ has
no such correction. It is Pearson's coefficient computed on mid-ranks, and a tie block
simply receives a common mid-rank that then enters a sum of squares as an ordinary value.

The comparators used below do not present the same tie structure, and the consequence
differs.

\paragraph{Ties on both sides: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$}
Both are durations and both can be censored at $H$. Censored runs form a tie block on the
$y$ side. $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is never censored ($0.0000$), but it is discrete and takes
only $321$ distinct values over $2\,000$ runs, so it carries tie blocks of its own.
Equation~\eqref{eq:taub} corrects on both sides and the compensation is complete: the
coefficient is normalised by exactly the set of orderable pairs.

\paragraph{Ties on one side: $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ against $A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$}
These two are defined on every run and never censored: $2\,000$ values out of $2\,000$,
with $1\,389$ and $1\,544$ distinct values respectively. There is no tie block on their
side at all, so the correction applies to the $x$ side only. The denominator still counts
the orderable pairs, but the two sides are no longer symmetric: ties in
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ remove pairs from the numerator without any counterpart, and the
coefficient is displaced \emph{towards zero} relative to a tie-free measurement of the
same association.

That direction cuts both ways for the central result of \hyperref[sec:D3]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D3}}. A
discernible coefficient is if anything understated; but a coefficient indistinguishable
from zero could in principle be a real association pulled towards zero by the ties. On this
campaign the effect is negligible either way: within a stratum of the decision domain,
only $0.2\%$ to $4.5\%$ of the pairs are tied on $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$, which caps
$|\tau_b|$ at $0.977$ or more.

\subsection{A Ten-Run Counter-Example, and the Configuration That Hides the Gap}
\label{sec:d2:contre}

Ten fictitious runs on a common horizon $H = 2\,000$: four observed, six censored. The
natural first configuration orders the four observed values perfectly and ties the six
censored runs on both sides. It yields $\rho = \tau_b = 1.000000$ exactly: the gap is
zero and, so built, the counter-example demonstrates nothing.

The reason is sharper than ``the example is badly chosen''. Ties on both sides require
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ itself to be censored, and on this campaign it never is:
\texttt{censored\_arf} $= 0.0000$ on all $2\,000$ runs. The configuration under which the
two coefficients coincide cannot occur in the data the example is meant to illustrate.

Two things separate the three rows, and they should not be conflated. What removes the gap
entirely is the tie structure: as soon as $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is left uncensored, which
is the situation that actually holds, the two coefficients part company by $0.071$ even
with the observed runs perfectly ordered. What then amplifies the gap is the ordering of
the observed runs: with four anti-ordered observations, Spearman reports $0.751$ where
$\tau_b$ reports $0.490$, a gap of $0.261$, nearly four times the ordered case.

The mechanism is the same in both rows: the six censored runs share a mid-rank that
enters Spearman's sums of squares as an ordinary value, while $\tau_b$ removes those pairs
from both counts. Ties on both sides hide this, because they cancel the effect exactly.
The size of the gap is not, by itself, evidence of an artefact: the two coefficients live
on different scales even without any tie. For a bivariate normal law with correlation $r$,
$\tau = (2/\pi)\arcsin r$ and $\rho_S = (6/\pi)\arcsin(r/2)$, so that $\rho_S$ runs about
$1.5$ times $\tau$ for weak associations.

\paragraph{Conclusion} Pearson is excluded by definition rather than by degree, since
censored runs carry no numerical value. Spearman is computable, but its value
has no direct reading and its handling of the tie blocks created by the censoring is
implicit. Kendall's $\tau_b$ is used throughout what follows because it reads directly on
pairs --- concordant minus discordant, among the pairs that can be ordered --- and corrects
for ties explicitly. Censored runs are imputed to the last rank, which is legitimate
because the horizon is common to all runs and only for that reason.

\section{Stratified Coefficients in Full}
\label{sec:D3}
% =====================================================================

Every coefficient here is Kendall's $\tau_b$ with censored runs imputed to the last rank,
justified in \hyperref[sec:D2]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D2}}. Every confidence interval on a $\tau_b$ is a bootstrap
interval over the seeds of the stratum, $2\,000$ resamples, quantiles $2.5\%$ and
$97.5\%$; every interval on a fraction is a Wilson $95\%$ interval. Values are produced by
\texttt{scripts/chiffres\_QD.py} and \texttt{derives\_QD.py}.

\subsection{Why Stratify}

$\Delta e$ drives both sides of every correlation of interest: it shortens
$\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ and it raises the evidence available to the detector. A coefficient
computed on the pooled $2\,000$ runs therefore measures the common dependence on
$\Delta e$ rather than any association between the two quantities at a fixed operating
point. Stratification removes that common cause. Each stratum holds $100$ seeds, and the
amplitude is constant inside it by construction.

\subsection{The Decision Rule, and Its Status}
\label{sec:D4rule}

\paragraph{A reservation, stated rather than glossed} A decision criterion should be
written down before any correlation is inspected. That anteriority cannot honestly be
claimed here. The pre-registered document of this work fixes the criteria of a
\emph{benchmark}: the tests that qualify candidate replacements for $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$
against an analytic reference. It contains neither the indicator evaluated here, nor its
comparators, nor the decision domain $\Delta e \ge 0.10$, nor the threshold on $\tau_b$,
nor the rule on confidence-interval bounds. The columns
\texttt{dans\_domaine\_decision}, \texttt{seuil\_detectabilite} and \texttt{discernable}
are produced by the same analysis pass as the coefficients they qualify. Anteriority is
therefore not claimed for this criterion. It is fixed here, applied uniformly, and no cell
is reclassified afterwards.

\paragraph{Two incompatible rules} Two rules for calling an association discernible
coexist in this work.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Threshold rule:} $|\tau_b| > 0.13295637$, the value in the
    \texttt{seuil\_detectabilite} column, constant across the table. It is quoted exactly
    rather than rounded, since a rounded threshold would not reproduce the column.
  \item \textbf{Interval rule:} the bootstrap confidence interval excludes zero. A $\tau_b$
    whose interval contains zero does not count.
\end{itemize}

They disagree on exactly three cells out of the $108$ in the decision domain
(\hyperref[tab:regles]{Table~\ref*{tab:regles}}).

the interval rule uses what the bootstrap actually provides. The threshold is also a
function of $n$ alone, so it says nothing about the tie structure of the particular pair
being tested, and \hyperref[sec:D2]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:D2}} established that this structure differs
systematically between the $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(q)$ comparators and the never-censored
$A(H)$ and $S_{\max}(H)$. Finally, the pre-registered benchmark already uses the interval
rule, so adopting it keeps one rule across the project instead of two.

\paragraph{$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ fails the group's own criterion} The criterion
adopted for the benchmark requires censoring of at most $5\%$ over the declared domain.
Inside the decision domain, $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ reaches $54\%$ at
$\Delta e = 0.141$, exceeding the criterion by a factor of $10.8$. Its worst value on the
full grid is $78\%$, at $\Delta e = 0.085$, but that amplitude lies outside the decision
domain and the figure is quoted as a grid measurement rather than as a breach of an
in-domain criterion. Its coefficients are published above because suppressing them would
hide the comparator that most stresses the method, but they are published with this
failure stated: at $\Delta e = 0.141$, the lowest amplitude of the decision domain, the
$\tau_b$ of $\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$ rests on a minority of observed runs and is not
quoted as evidence.

\paragraph{No median delay beyond $50\%$ censoring} At $\lambda = 25$ and $\lambda = 50$
no median detection delay is reported anywhere in this report, and at $\lambda = 8$ none
is reported at $\Delta e = 0.028$ where censoring reaches $90\%$. A median computed on the
surviving runs excludes the slowest ones by construction and is not a median of the
quantity of interest.

\paragraph{Does censoring drive the results? No} Each coefficient was recomputed twice,
once with censored runs imputed to the last rank and once on complete cases only, then
compared cell by cell (\hyperref[tab:cascomplets]{Table~\ref*{tab:cascomplets}}, \hyperref[fig:imputation]{Figure~\ref*{fig:imputation}}). For the
three never-censored comparators the two computations agree exactly, to $0.000000$, which
is what they must do and serves as a control on the implementation. For the two materially
censored comparators the largest disagreement anywhere in the table is $0.0403$, on
$\tau_{\mathrm{swap}}(75\%)$, an order of magnitude below the coefficients that carry the
conclusions above and well inside their confidence intervals. The imputation convention of
\hyperref[sec:d2:rang]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d2:rang}} could be replaced by complete-case analysis without changing a
verdict.

\subsection{Reservations, and What Is Not Addressed}

\paragraph{Multiplicity} The decision domain contains $108$ cells, of which $68$ are
significant at the $5\%$ level with no correction for multiplicity. The signal for
$S_{\max}(H)$ above $\Delta e = 0.287$ is far too systematic to be a multiplicity artefact,
running to $15$ consecutive amplitudes in the same direction
(\hyperref[fig:forest]{Figure~\ref*{fig:forest}}), but individual cells whose $|\tau_b|$ is near the detectability
threshold are not quoted on their own.

\paragraph{The return time is not correlated run by run} Next to the residual error,
the natural second comparator for $\tau_{\mathrm{ARF}}$ is the effective time of return
to normal, $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$. It is not computed as a per-run correlation anywhere, and the reason belongs here rather than in silence.
$\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is defined only on the seed-averaged error curve, one value per
amplitude: on a single run the binary sequence is far too noisy for a threshold crossing to
signal genuine recovery. The correlations above are computed run by run, so the two objects
are not commensurable. It appears in \hyperref[fig:joint]{Figure~\ref*{fig:joint}} as the $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}$
marker, the only use its definition supports, dashed at the amplitudes where the test has
no power to resolve it.

``Return of the error to an acceptable level'' also fails as an adaptation criterion, for
the reason \hyperref[sec:budget]{Section~\ref*{sec:budget}} establishes. At $\Delta e = 0.498$ the threshold
$\hat p_0 + \rho\,\Delta e$ is crossed at step $46$, with or without the persistence
requirement, because the post-drift problem is easier than the original one and the error
falls below the pre-drift baseline. A criterion that a simplified problem satisfies
immediately does not measure adaptation.

\paragraph{Scope} Everything above is measured at $M = 10$ on one campaign, so no result
of this section holds as a function of forest size; the instrumented runs at other values
of $M$ were produced for a different test and carry no evidence trace. No result involves
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}}$ at $\lambda = 25$ or $\lambda = 50$ as a correlated variable, for the
censoring reason given above. Harrell's $C$ index was not used, because the common-horizon
argument of \hyperref[sec:d2:rang]{Appendix~\ref*{sec:d2:rang}} makes rank imputation licit here and the
complete-case comparison shows the choice is immaterial; under a variable horizon that
reasoning would fail and the $C$ index would be required.
